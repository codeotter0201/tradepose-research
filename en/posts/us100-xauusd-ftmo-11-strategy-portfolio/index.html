US100 and gold: an 11-strategy FTMO 100K portfolio, its sizing, full metrics and drawdowns

This article combines two finished studies into one FTMO 100K portfolio: the six US100 weekday clock × Market Profile strategies already live on LU’s account, and the five 1-hour SuperTrend strategies on US100 and gold. Entries and exits stay as they were; only the sizing is new: every one of the 11 strategies loses the same amount at its stop. All performance comes from the TradePose Trades Analyzer at this sizing, after round-trip costs. These are backtest results, not a trading record. Trading terms (Buy Limit, Buy Stop, stop, breakeven, trailing stop) are in the glossary.

Conclusion

Each group has its own strengths: LU’s six strategies have a profit factor of 2.20 and shallow drawdowns; the five SuperTrend strategies trade more, on US100 and gold. Their daily results correlate only -0.03, and the 11 strategies pairwise between -0.09 and 0.22, so together the profits add up while the drawdowns do not: the portfolio’s max drawdown of $2,791 is shallower than SuperTrend’s $3,046 alone, and the recovery factor rises from 26.5 and 20.0 to 39.9. Six weight designs were compared; the simplest, all equal, was kept: out of sample in 2025–2026 it did about as well as the most estimated design, and it estimates nothing.

That is the conclusion. Each section below is the evidence for one claim; follow the sections or the evidence links into the strategy report for the data on every parameter set.

Why 400? First the largest base that keeps every stop on one day within 80% of FTMO’s daily loss limit (USD 3,600): USD 320. That day is Monday, when LU’s four Monday strategies and all five SuperTrend strategies can trade. The worst day the backtest actually had was only −$876, and the max drawdown used a third of the limit, so the size is scaled by 1.25 to USD 400:

Why do the two groups fit together?

  • Different hours: LU’s strategies trade Sunday evening into Monday, Monday evening and Wednesday evening, mostly outside the New York day; SuperTrend’s intraday strategies trade the New York day, its held strategies enter in the day.
  • Different markets: LU is all US100; SuperTrend has three gold strategies.
  • Daily correlation -0.03: the groups are almost uncorrelated; the 11 strategies pairwise between -0.09 and 0.22.
  • Result: losing months fall from more than ten for each group alone to 6; the recovery factor of 39.9 is higher than either group alone.

Questions this study answers

What is the portfolio?

LU’s six live US100 weekday clock × Market Profile strategies plus five 1-hour SuperTrend strategies on US100 and gold; parameters and exits are those of the original studies.

How large a position for an FTMO 100K account?

Every one of the 11 strategies loses USD 400 at its stop; lots = 400 ÷ (stop multiple × daily volatility at entry × point value). Of six weight designs, all equal did as well out of sample as the best and estimates nothing; 400 is 1.25 × the 320 that keeps every stop on one day within USD 3,600.

How did it perform?

From 2022-06 to 2026-10: $111,273 in total, max drawdown $2,791, recovery factor 39.9, daily Sharpe 3.83; every year made money and the worst day was −$1,100.

Are the two groups better together?

Yes. They correlate -0.03 day by day, so profits add and drawdowns do not: the portfolio’s max drawdown is shallower than SuperTrend’s alone, its recovery factor higher than either group’s, and only 6 months lost money.

Limits

  • This is a backtest: parameters were chosen on 2022–2024 data, 2025–2026 is partly out of sample; live slippage, fills and quote delays can make results worse.
  • Every stop on one day above the daily limit never happened, but its chance is not zero; a gap can also lose a little more than the stop (at most 1.1× the budget per stop-out in the backtest).
  • The SuperTrend strategies’ ATR is a recursive average, so its early values differ by a few percent with the start of the data and agree from 2025 on; the SuperTrend numbers here differ by 1–2% from the original study.
  • The page’s statistics exclude swap; swap is listed under Costs above.
