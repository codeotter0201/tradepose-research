Do Market Profile signals work on both sides of value? 16 US100 events and their mirrors

This is the root of the Market Profile signals series: the later delayed entry, filter, SL/TP, breakeven and portfolio studies all build on the signals chosen here. A Market Profile signal is only a Profile signal if it works on both sides of the value area. If “buy when price reclaims VAL” is a real effect, its mirror, “sell when price falls back below VAH”, should work too; if only one side works, the result may just follow the direction the market tends to move at that time of day. This study adds the four missing mirror events to 12 Market Profile events, measures every trade in volatility mode (P&L divided by the daily ATR at the time of the trade), and asks which signals hold on both sides of value, which only follow the session’s direction, and whether either beats entering at the same time without any signal (the control).

Conclusion

Only partly. Before 09:30, many event pairs make money only on the short side, above and below value alike, which is a session-direction bias rather than a Profile effect. From 09:30 on, the pairs that do hold on both sides mostly point to continuation away from value: short below the value area, long above it. Reversion back into value shows up mostly before 09:30; after 09:30 only the false break of VAL / VAH (G03 / G12; codes and conditions in the glossary) holds as reversion on both sides, at 10:30. Three of the four priority signals follow the continuation pattern; all four beat the unconditional entry at the same time and keep a profit factor above 1 when the ATR length changes, but they were picked from 180 parameter sets and are not yet a proven edge.

Why test each event against its mirror?

Running every event in both directions and keeping whichever direction made money is not enough. Without costs, a short on an event is exactly the opposite of a long on the same event, so that choice only tells us which sign the result has, not why. A mirror is the same rule flipped across the Profile: VAL becomes VAH, the profile low becomes the profile high, “below the POC” becomes “above the POC”. A Profile hypothesis such as “a failed break returns to value” predicts a long on the event and a short on its mirror; the opposite hypothesis, “price that leaves value keeps going”, predicts the reverse. If an event and its mirror both work only long (or only short), the result follows the direction of the session, whatever the side of value.

The conditions and mirror pairs of the 12 original events (G01–G12) are in the strategy code table of the glossary. Eight event variants sit below or at the value area and five above it, and four events had no mirror. This study adds them:

  • G13: exactly N (1 or 2) bars close above VAH, then a bar closes back below it, mirroring G04 below VAL.
  • G14: the previous bar breaks above the profile high and closes back below it, mirroring G05.
  • G15: the previous bar breaks above the POC and closes back below it, mirroring G07.
  • G16: the previous bar breaks above VAL and closes back below it, mirroring G11 at VAH.

Hover over a signal in the strategy report to see its exact condition. The mirror events were checked against an independent recomputation on a two-month sample, with no mismatches.

Why measure in volatility mode?

Each trade’s result is divided by the volatility when it was traded: the previous completed New York daily ATR over 72 daily bars. The Nasdaq 100 moves far more points per day at 30,000 than at 15,000, and its volatility relative to price also changes from year to year, so a percentage return gives quiet periods and volatile periods different weights. In volatility mode every trade is measured against the same yardstick, as if each one were sized for the same volatility risk. The unit changes which parameter sets look best, so it has to be fixed before the signals are picked, and every layer of this series uses the same unit.

72 daily bars is about 60 trading days: the New York daily series counts Sunday evening’s short session as its own bar, about one bar in six. Because that bar’s range is small, this ATR is about 10% below an ATR of weekdays only, a steady offset that does not change rankings.

Does the length of the ATR change the result?

The volatility unit depends on how many days the ATR averages. We reran the first choices and runners-up with daily ATRs over about 20, 40 and 120 trading days. The priority signals keep their profit factor above 1 at every length:

G13 N2 long 09:302.3382.8822.4932.8822.6172.8822.7452.794

Over the same trades, 20, 40 and 60 days give almost the same profit factor. The 120-day ATR is only known from late April 2022, so its sample is a few months shorter and its profit factors move a little either way, all still above 1. Across all 180 parameter sets, the profit-factor ranking under each length stays close to the ranking under 60 days, and almost all first choices stay the same. 60 days sits in the middle: long enough that one volatile week does not swing the unit, short enough to follow the move from the 2022 bear market to the calmer years after it. The glossary explains why we use a daily ATR.

What happens on Mondays, and how does the next layer choose?

This study leaves Mondays out because they behave differently: entering long at any of these times on a Monday and holding to 16:00 made money far more reliably than on any other weekday, whether or not the Profile said anything. Picking signals on every weekday without accounting for that would mostly pick Mondays. Weekdays differ in the other direction too: Thursdays leaned clearly short.

The next layer of the research therefore uses every weekday, but ranks each trade net of the session drift: from each trade we subtract the average result of entering at the same weekday, the same 15-minute bar and in the same direction, holding to 16:00. The pick rule is otherwise unchanged. Each later layer, from delayed entry to the portfolio and position sizing, is a separate article and is measured in volatility mode.

Read with caution:These parameter sets are the best results picked from 180 after looking at the data, so their averages overstate true performance. Mondays behave differently (longs are clearly better) and are left out of the main sample. There is no independent validation period, and costs are not included. This is not trading advice.

What does this mean?

On US100, Market Profile signals that hold on both sides of value point to continuation, not reversion: after 09:30, trading away from the value area worked above and below it alike, while before 09:30 much of the apparent edge is a short bias of the session. The next layer should build on the four priority signals and the continuation pairs, and compare each against its mirror and the same-time control rather than against zero.

Questions this study answers

Do Market Profile events hold on both sides of the value area, or do they only follow the session's direction?

Only part of the result is a Profile effect. Before 09:30, many events and their mirrors make money only on the short side, which is a short bias of the session; from 09:30 on, the pairs that hold on both sides mostly point to continuation away from value (short below, long above). Reversion back into value shows up mostly before 09:30; after 09:30 only the false break of VAL / VAH (G03 / G12) holds as reversion on both sides, at 10:30.

Which signals beat entering at the same time without a signal?

The four priority signals: the open below VAL (G01) short at 10:30, the open above VAH (G09) long at 10:30, one bar closing above VAH and falling back (G13 N1) long at 10:30, and two bars closing above VAH and falling back (G13 N2) long at 09:30. Each earns more per trade than the unconditional entry at the same time and direction. For example, an unconditional long at 10:30 earns +0.003 vol+2.07 bps per trade, while the open above VAH (G09) long earns +0.025 vol+5.77 bps. They were picked from 180 parameter sets and are not yet a proven edge.

Questions raised along the way

Running each event long and short cannot separate a Profile effect from the session's direction. What did the study do?

It added mirrors for the four events that had none (G13–G16) and tested every event together with the same rule flipped across the value area. A real Profile effect should hold on both sides; when an event and its mirror both work only in the same direction, the result is classed as the session’s direction.

Percentage returns weight quiet and volatile years differently. Which unit should the study use?

Volatility mode: each trade’s P&L is divided by the previous completed New York daily ATR (72 daily bars, about 60 trading days), as if every trade carried the same volatility risk. With the ATR over about 20, 40 or 120 trading days instead, the priority signals keep a profit factor above 1 and the ranking of the 180 parameter sets stays close.

An unconditional long at these times is unusually good on Mondays. How does signal selection handle that?

This study leaves Mondays out of the main sample, so the chosen signals are not just Mondays. From the next layer on, the sample includes every weekday, but each trade is ranked after subtracting the average result of entering on the same weekday, at the same 15-minute bar and in the same direction, held to 16:00.

Signal days already lean in one direction. Does that overstate what the signal adds?

A same-time control on event days checks this: on the days the G01 short at 10:30 later fired, an unconditional short at 10:30 earns +0.155 vol+25.05 bps per trade, against +0.034 vol+4.53 bps for G01 itself. That control is not tradable, because which day the event fires is only known once it fires; it shows how much of the day’s move happens before the event confirms it.
