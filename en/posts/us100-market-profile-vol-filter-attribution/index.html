Where is the information in a trend filter? SuperTrend, ATR alone and the joint pick on 36 US100 Market Profile setups

This is layer 3 of the Market Profile signals series: filter attribution. The previous article is layer 2, Which SuperTrend filter, in which volatility?, which picked a SuperTrend veto that only applies in one volatility state (ST × volatility below); this layer puts it together with layer 1, delayed entry, and changes only which veto is used, keeping the signals and the hold to 16:00. G01–G16 are Market Profile event codes (for example, G10 = a whole bar closes above VAH); the glossary lists every condition, and P&L is in volatility mode. The question: is the information in the SuperTrend, or simply in the volatility state? This layer compares four vetoes (none, SuperTrend always, ST × volatility, the volatility state alone) on each of the 36 setups of the root study, each with the delayed entry re-chosen on the days it keeps, tests every veto against random timing, and picks the parents that go on to the stop-loss layer.

Conclusion

On average SuperTrend × volatility is the best of the four: the highest mean recovery factor, and it beats random timing on 30 of 36 setups. But the split by direction matters. For longs the SuperTrend carries the information; for shorts the low-volatility state alone does about as well, and ATR alone matches or beats it on 12 of 22 short setups. Per-setup choices are unstable: picking the veto on 2022–2024 beats no veto in 2025–2026 on only 17 of 36 setups. So the research keeps one rule for all setups, ST × volatility, and 21 setups pass as parents for the stop-loss layer.

  1. Mean recovery factor with the chosen entry: 2.031.77 with ST × volatility, against 1.161.15 with no veto and immediate entry. Evidence: Returns
  2. For G10 short at 08:30 the volatility state alone gives a recovery factor of 3.482.67, against 3.172.31 with the SuperTrend added. Evidence: Parameter stability
  3. Every veto lowers the mean max drawdown, from −12.2 vol−24.1% without a veto to −9.5 vol−19.6% with ST × volatility. Evidence: Risk

That is the conclusion. Each section below is the evidence for one claim; follow the sections or the evidence links into the strategy report for the data on every parameter set.

How are the vetoes compared?

Each veto decides which signal days are traded. On those days the delayed entry is chosen again with the score of the delayed-entry layer, so a veto is never judged with an entry tuned for other days. A veto’s recovery factor is then compared with the same day mask shifted around the calendar by every offset from 5% to 95% of the signal days: the same number of skipped days, only at the wrong times.

Is the information in the SuperTrend or in the volatility state?

It depends on the direction. For shorts, skipping low-volatility days alone does about as well as adding the SuperTrend; for longs the SuperTrend matters. Setups where ATR alone matches or beats ST × volatility, both with their chosen entries:

Why not pick the best veto per setup?

Picked per setup, ATR alone would be chosen most often (13 times), then ST × volatility (11). But the pick does not carry over: chosen on 2022–2024, it beats no veto in 2025–2026 on only 17 of 36 setups, close to a coin flip. A per-setup maximum over noisy recovery factors mostly picks noise. The research therefore keeps one rule for every setup, the matched ST × volatility veto, which is also the best on average and the most consistent against random timing.

What does this mean?

A trend filter’s value depends on the side: for these longs the SuperTrend itself is informative, for these shorts the low-volatility state carries most of it. The 21 parents go on to layer 4: SL/TP.

Questions this study answers

Is a trend filter's information in the SuperTrend or only in the volatility state?

It depends on the direction. For longs the SuperTrend carries the information; for shorts skipping low-volatility days alone does about as well, with ATR alone matching or beating ST × volatility on 12 of 22 short setups. For G10 short at 08:30 the volatility state alone gives a recovery factor of 3.482.67, against 3.172.31 with the SuperTrend added.

Which setups go on to the SL/TP layer with their filter and delayed entry?

One rule for every setup (ST × volatility with the re-chosen delayed entry): the 21 setups with positive expectancy in both 2022–2024 and 2025–2026 and a veto beating more than half of its circular shifts become parents. It is also the best of the four vetoes on average, with a mean recovery factor of 2.031.77 against 1.161.15 for no veto with immediate entry.

Questions raised along the way

A veto changes which days are traded. Is it fair to keep a delayed entry tuned on all days?

No, so under each veto the delayed entry is re-chosen on the days the veto keeps, with the layer 1 score, and the immediate entry is shown alongside for comparison.

Would each setup do better with its own best veto?

The pick does not carry over. Picked per setup, ATR alone is chosen most often (13 times), but a veto picked on 2022–2024 beats no veto in 2025–2026 on only 17 of 36 setups, close to a coin flip. A per-setup maximum over noisy recovery factors mostly picks noise, so one rule is kept for all setups.

Why do 15 setups fail as parents?

Three only because their veto does not beat the random shifts, twelve because they lose money in one of the two halves, mostly shorts in 2022–2024.

Vetoes
None; ST always (trade only when the direction's 1h SuperTrend agrees); ST × volatility (the matched veto of the filter layer: long skips a bearish 1h SuperTrend only when daily volatility is high, short skips a bullish one only when it is low); ATR only (skip the same volatility state whatever the SuperTrend says)
Entry
Under each veto, the delayed entry is re-chosen on the days the veto keeps, with the delayed-entry layer's score; immediate entry shown alongside
Veto test
Recovery factor of the veto with its entry against every circular shift of its day mask by 5%–95% of the signal days
Pick per setup
Highest recovery among no veto and the vetoes beating more than half of their shifts
Parents for SL/TP
ST × volatility with its entry, positive expectancy in 2022–2024 and 2025–2026, beating more than half of its shifts
Unit
Volatility mode: P&L divided by the previous completed New York daily ATR (72 daily bars)
Sample
New York Base entries from late January 2022 to September 23, 2026, Monday to Friday

Not included
