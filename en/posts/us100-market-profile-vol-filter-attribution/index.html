Where is the information in a trend filter? SuperTrend, ATR alone and the joint pick on 36 US100 Market Profile setups

This is layer 3 of the Market Profile signals series: filter attribution. The previous article is layer 2, Which SuperTrend filter, in which volatility?, which picked one SuperTrend veto per direction: for longs the daily SuperTrend in every volatility state, for shorts the 1h SuperTrend only on low-volatility days (both called ST × volatility below). This layer puts it together with layer 1, delayed entry, and changes only which veto is used, keeping the signals and the hold to 16:00. G01–G16 are Market Profile event codes (for example, G10 = a whole bar closes above VAH); the glossary lists every condition, and P&L is in volatility mode. The question: is the information in the SuperTrend, or simply in the volatility state? This layer compares four vetoes (none, SuperTrend always, ST × volatility, the volatility state alone) on each of the 36 setups of the root study, each with the delayed entry re-chosen on the days it keeps, tests every veto against random timing, and picks the parents that go on to the stop-loss layer.

Conclusion

The answer differs by direction. For shorts the low-volatility state carries most of the information: skipping low-volatility days alone gives the highest mean recovery factor on the short side and matches or beats ST × volatility on 11 of 22 short setups. For longs the filter layer chose the daily SuperTrend in every volatility state, so there is no volatility state to isolate: ATR only has nothing to veto and trades every day. The daily SuperTrend beats random timing on 11 of 14 long setups and lowers the drawdown, but raises the recovery factor over no veto on only 6. Per-setup choices are unstable: picking the veto on 2022–2024 beats no veto in 2025–2026 on only 16 of 36 setups, against 27 for the matched veto chosen the same way. So the research keeps one rule for all setups, ST × volatility, and 19 setups pass as parents for the stop-loss layer.

  1. Mean recovery factor with the chosen entry: 1.621.58 with ST × volatility and 1.871.81 with ATR only, against 1.301.34 with no veto and immediate entry. Evidence: Returns
  2. For G02 short at 10:30 the volatility state alone gives a recovery factor of 4.323.28, against 1.481.14 with the SuperTrend added. Evidence: Parameter stability
  3. Every veto lowers the mean max drawdown, from −11.7 vol−23.7% without a veto to −8.7 vol−18.0% with ST × volatility. Evidence: Risk

That is the conclusion. Each section below is the evidence for one claim; follow the sections or the evidence links into the strategy report for the data on every parameter set.

How are the vetoes compared?

Each veto decides which signal days are traded. On those days the delayed entry is chosen again with the score of the delayed-entry layer, so a veto is never judged with an entry tuned for other days. A veto’s recovery factor is then compared with the same day mask shifted around the calendar by every offset from 5% to 95% of the signal days: the same number of skipped days, only at the wrong times.

Against random timing, ST × volatility beats more than half of its shifts on 29 of 36 setups (11 of 14 longs, 18 of 22 shorts), SuperTrend always on 24, ATR only on 16, all of them shorts: for longs ATR only trades every day, and a mask that keeps every day cannot beat its own shifts.

Is the information in the SuperTrend or in the volatility state?

It depends on the direction, and the two directions ask different questions.

For shorts the filter layer chose a veto that acts only on low-volatility days (skip a bullish 1h SuperTrend only then), so the volatility state can be tested on its own. Skipping low-volatility days alone does about as well as adding the SuperTrend: ATR only matches or beats ST × volatility on 11 of 22 short setups. Some of them, both with their chosen entries:

Why not pick the best veto per setup?

Picked per setup, no veto would be chosen most often (12 times: 8 longs, 4 shorts), then ATR only (9, all shorts); the SuperTrend vetoes take the other 15 (for longs the daily SuperTrend 6 times, for shorts SuperTrend always 5 times and ST × volatility 4). But the pick does not carry over: chosen on 2022–2024, it beats no veto in 2025–2026 on only 16 of 36 setups. The matched ST × volatility veto, with its entry chosen on 2022–2024 the same way, beats no veto in 2025–2026 on 27 of 36. A per-setup maximum over noisy recovery factors mostly picks noise. The research therefore keeps one rule for every setup, the matched ST × volatility veto, which is also the most consistent against random timing.

What does this mean?

A trend filter’s value depends on the side: for these shorts the low-volatility state carries most of it; for these longs the daily SuperTrend skips better days than chance and cuts the drawdown, but raises the recovery factor over trading every day on fewer than half of the setups. The 19 parents go on to layer 4: SL/TP.

Questions this study answers

Is a trend filter's information in the SuperTrend or only in the volatility state?

It depends on the direction. For shorts skipping low-volatility days alone does about as well, with ATR only matching or beating ST × volatility on 11 of 22 short setups; for G02 short at 10:30 the volatility state alone gives a recovery factor of 4.323.28, against 1.481.14 with the SuperTrend added. For longs the veto is the daily SuperTrend in every volatility state, so the volatility state alone has nothing to veto; the daily SuperTrend beats random timing on 11 of 14 long setups and lowers the drawdown, but raises the recovery factor over no veto on only 6.

Which setups go on to the SL/TP layer with their filter and delayed entry?

One rule for every setup (ST × volatility with the re-chosen delayed entry): the 19 setups with positive expectancy in both 2022–2024 and 2025–2026 and a veto beating more than half of its circular shifts become parents. It is the veto that beats random timing most often (29 of 36 setups), with a mean recovery factor of 1.621.58 against 1.301.34 for no veto with immediate entry.

Questions raised along the way

A veto changes which days are traded. Is it fair to keep a delayed entry tuned on all days?

No, so under each veto the delayed entry is re-chosen on the days the veto keeps, with the layer 1 score, and the immediate entry is shown alongside for comparison.

Would each setup do better with its own best veto?

The pick does not carry over. Picked per setup, no veto is chosen most often (12 times), then ATR only (9), but a veto picked on 2022–2024 beats no veto in 2025–2026 on only 16 of 36 setups, while the matched ST × volatility veto chosen the same way does so on 27. A per-setup maximum over noisy recovery factors mostly picks noise, so one rule is kept for all setups.

Why do 17 setups fail as parents?

Three only because their veto does not beat the random shifts, fourteen because they lose money in one of the two halves, mostly shorts in 2022–2024.
