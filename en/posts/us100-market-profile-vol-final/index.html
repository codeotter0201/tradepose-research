From signal to final strategy: 11 US100 Market Profile setups, layer by layer

This is the conclusion of the Market Profile signals series. The series starts from the root signals and adds a delayed entry, a trend filter, a stop loss and target, and a breakeven stop, one change per layer (each layer’s conclusion is under “What did each layer do?” below). Eleven setups came through every layer. G01–G16 are Market Profile event codes (for example, G09 = the open is above VAH); the glossary lists every condition, and P&L is in volatility mode. This article runs them once more in a single backtest, stage by stage, so each step’s contribution can be seen, compares the last stage with and without the veto, and splits the results into Mondays and the other weekdays.

Conclusion

Every one of the eleven final strategies has a lower drawdown, a higher recovery factor and a higher profit factor than its raw signal. The stop loss is the one stage that lowers the drawdown and raises the recovery factor of all eleven; the veto makes the largest single cut in drawdown for six of them, and the delayed entry, once the veto is in, lowers the recovery factor of five longs. At the last stage the veto raises the profit factor and the recovery factor of all eleven. Mondays split the set: G08 short at 08:30 and G09 short at 06:30 lose on Mondays at every stage, the two G11 longs lose on Mondays once the stop is in, and the two 08:30 setups, G04 N1 long and G05 short, do better on Mondays. Four of the eleven are events the root study added for the mirror test; two of them have fewer than 100 trades.

  1. G09 short at 06:30 (the open is above VAH) goes from a recovery factor of 0.06−0.21 as a raw signal to 10.097.10 as a final strategy, with a max drawdown of −3.3 vol−6.9%. Evidence: Risk
  2. Without the veto G14 long at 09:30 (a false break above the profile high) ends with a recovery factor of 0.06−0.33; with it, 3.222.70. Evidence: Returns
  3. G09 short’s final strategy has a profit factor of 0.930.91 on Mondays and 1.701.59 on Tuesday–Friday. Evidence: Consistency over time

That is the conclusion. Each section below is the evidence for one claim; follow the sections or the evidence links into the strategy report for the data on every parameter set.

What did each layer do?

Each layer changes one thing on the setups the previous layer chose, and picks its parameters with its own rule and measure:

  • Root signals, Do Market Profile signals work on both sides of value?: every Market Profile event is tested together with its mirror on the other side of the value area and against a control that enters at the same time without a signal. Events that hold on both sides mostly point to continuation away from value; many early-session shorts that look good only follow the session’s short bias.
  • Layer 1: delayed entry, Can a delayed entry cut MAE?: compares the favorable delayed entry (Buy/Sell Limit, a long places a limit order below the signal price and waits for a pullback) with the adverse delayed entry (Buy/Sell Stop, a long places a stop order above the signal price and waits for a breakout). A delayed entry improves 12 of the 36 setups and for 8 of them the entry picked on the earlier part of the sample still helps in the later part; six of those eight use the favorable delayed entry, paid for with fewer filled signals.
  • Layer 2: trend filter, Which SuperTrend filter, in which volatility?: skips a day when the SuperTrend opposes the trade, checked against circular shifts of the skipped days to rule out luck. The rule picks the daily SuperTrend for longs, in every volatility state, and the 1-hour SuperTrend for shorts, vetoing them only when daily volatility is low.
  • Layer 3: filter attribution, Where is the information in a trend filter?: looks at the veto and the delayed entry together. The best filter differs from setup to setup (no filter for 12, the SuperTrend alone for 11, the volatility state alone for 9, both for 4), so one rule is kept for every setup, and 19 parents go on to the stop-loss layer.
  • Layer 4: SL/TP, Where to put the stop and the target?: the stop is set from each parent’s own MAE, beyond the bulk of the trades, and kept only on a parameter plateau. Eleven of the 19 parents get a robust stop, and every one of them makes the drawdown shallower; three also keep a take profit.
  • Layer 5: breakeven stop, Where should breakeven trigger?: the trigger sits only where losing trades rarely reach. Eight of the eleven keep one; G09 long at 10:30 and G14 long at 06:30 gain the most.

How these eleven setups trade together, with correlation, spread and slippage, and position size on an FTMO account, is in the series portfolio article, Portfolio and position size.

What are the final parameters?

Distances are multiples of the previous completed New York daily ATR(72). A trade that has not exited earlier takes a time exit at 16:00 New York time. The veto skips a long day when the daily SuperTrend (ATR 18 × 2.5) is bearish, whatever the volatility, and a short day when the 1-hour SuperTrend (ATR 460 × 1.5) is bullish and the daily volatility level is in its bottom third.

The Monday Market Profile starts at the same time on the previous Friday and covers Friday’s session and the Sunday evening session, consistent with the other days. Monday trades still differ: weekend news can open Monday farther from the previous value area than on other days, and the root study already found that an unconditional long at these times does unusually well on Mondays.

G08 short at 08:30 (a whole bar above the POC) and G09 short at 06:30 lose on Mondays at every stage, with or without the veto; Monday’s long drift works against them. The two G11 longs (the VAH retest holds) show another pattern: their raw signals make money on Mondays, but from the stop-loss stage on their Monday trades lose, while Tuesday–Friday improve. G04 N1 long and G05 short at 08:30 do better on Mondays at the last stage, on fewer than 30 Monday trades each. For G14 long at 06:30 the raw signal makes most of its money on Mondays; the later stages are what make Tuesday–Friday clearly profitable. G13 N1 long at 10:30 has only 10 Monday trades and is left out.

Every layer picked its parameters on the full Monday-to-Friday sample, but Mondays are only a small part of it, so the Tuesday-to-Friday results outweigh them, and the Monday difference does not go away just because Mondays are in the sample. In live trading some setups could skip Mondays, but that decision was made after seeing the results and needs confirmation on future data.

Are the mirror events better?

Four of the eleven setups use events the root study added for the mirror test: G13 N1 long at 10:30 (one bar above VAH, then back, mirroring G04 N1 below VAL), G14 long at 06:30 and 09:30 (a false break above the profile high, mirroring G05), and G16 short at 10:30 (the VAL retest fails, mirroring G11). G16 short and G11 long at 10:30 are a complete pair: both trade continuation away from value, and both reach the final set. G13 N1 long has the highest final profit factor in the set, 2.202.19, but only 7474 trades, and G14 long at 09:30 has 9393. G14 long at 06:30 has more trades but is the least consistent final strategy: positive in 33 of 55 years.

Read with caution:Each stage was chosen on the same 2022–2026 history, so the final results are in-sample. Costs are not included. G13 N1 long at 10:30 and G14 long at 09:30 have fewer than 100 trades. This is not trading advice.

What does this mean?

Layer by layer, the series turned eleven raw Market Profile signals into strategies with shallower drawdowns and higher recovery factors, mostly through the veto and the stop loss. Whether they hold up after spread and slippage, and how large each position can be on a prop-firm account, is the subject of the series portfolio article, Portfolio and position size.

Questions this study answers

How do the final strategies improve on the raw signals, and which step contributes most?

All eleven final strategies have a shallower drawdown and a higher recovery factor than their raw signal. The stop loss is the only step that improves all eleven, and the veto makes the largest single cut in drawdown for six: G09 short at 06:30 goes from a max drawdown of −18.6 vol−34.3% to −6.8 vol−11.8% with the veto alone, and from a recovery factor of 0.06−0.21 as a raw signal to 10.097.10 as a final strategy.

Is the SuperTrend × volatility veto still needed at the last stage?

Yes. The veto raises the profit factor and the recovery factor of all eleven at the last stage. G14 long at 09:30 ends with a recovery factor of 0.06−0.33 without the veto and 3.222.70 with it.

Can these strategies be traded on Mondays?

It depends on the setup. G08 short at 08:30 and G09 short at 06:30 lose on Mondays at every stage: G09 short’s final strategy has a profit factor of 0.930.91 on Mondays and 1.701.59 on Tuesday–Friday. The two G11 longs lose on Mondays once the stop is in. G04 N1 long and G05 short at 08:30 do better on Mondays.

Questions raised along the way

Each layer picked its parameters separately. Do they still hold in one backtest?

The final parameters are generated from each layer’s outputs, not picked from one large search. This article reruns the eleven setups in a single backtest, stage by stage, so each step is measured on its own: the veto alone helps nine and hurts the two 10:30 longs G09 and G13 N1, the delayed entry helps the shorts but lowers the recovery factor of five longs, and the stop loss improves all eleven.

The Monday profile is built the same way as on other days. Why are Mondays still different?

The Monday profile starts at the same time on the previous Friday and covers Friday’s session and the Sunday evening session; but weekend news can open Monday far from the previous value area, and Mondays are only a small part of the selection sample, outweighed by Tuesday to Friday. The study splits each final strategy’s trades into Mondays and Tuesday–Friday; skipping Mondays is a choice made after seeing the results and needs confirmation on future data.

G13 N1 long has the highest final profit factor. Should it come first?

Not yet. It has only 7474 trades, and in the root study it and its mirror G04 N1 both work only long, so its result looks more like the market’s direction after 10:30 than a side of the value area. The other mirror setups are mixed: G16 short at 10:30 completes a continuation pair with G11 long, while G14 long at 06:30 is positive in only 33 of 55 years.
