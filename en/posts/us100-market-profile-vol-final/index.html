From signal to final strategy: 9 US100 Market Profile setups, layer by layer

This is the conclusion of the Market Profile signals series. The series starts from the root signals and adds a delayed entry, a trend filter, a stop loss and target, and a breakeven stop, one change per layer (each layer’s conclusion is under “What did each layer do?” below). Nine setups came through every layer. G01–G16 are Market Profile event codes (for example, G09 = the open is above VAH); the glossary lists every condition, and P&L is in volatility mode. This article runs them once more in a single backtest, stage by stage, so each step’s contribution can be seen, compares the last stage with and without the veto, and splits the results into Mondays and the other weekdays.

Conclusion

Every one of the nine final strategies has a lower drawdown and a higher recovery factor than its raw signal, and the stop loss is the stage that does most of it. The veto raises the profit factor of all nine at the last stage, and matters most for the two 06:30 shorts. Mondays split the set: the 06:30 shorts lose on Mondays at every stage, while G05 short at 08:30 does better on Mondays. Four of the nine are events the root study added for the mirror test, but three of them rest on small samples.

  1. G09 short at 06:30 goes from a recovery factor of 0.06−0.14 as a raw signal to 9.817.18 as a final strategy, with a max drawdown of −3.3 vol−6.6%. Evidence: Risk
  2. Without the veto G15 short at 06:30 ends with a recovery factor of 0.580.06; with it, 2.341.30. Evidence: Returns
  3. G09 short’s final strategy has a profit factor of 0.910.89 on Mondays and 1.691.58 on Tuesday–Friday. Evidence: Consistency over time

That is the conclusion. Each section below is the evidence for one claim; follow the sections or the evidence links into the strategy report for the data on every parameter set.

What did each layer do?

Each layer changes one thing on the setups the previous layer chose, and picks its parameters with its own rule and measure:

  • Root signals, Do Market Profile signals work on both sides of value?: every Market Profile event is tested together with its mirror on the other side of the value area and against a control that enters at the same time without a signal. Events that hold on both sides mostly point to continuation away from value; many early-session shorts that look good only follow the session’s short bias.
  • Layer 1: delayed entry, Can a delayed entry cut MAE?: compares the favorable delayed entry (Buy/Sell Limit, a long places a limit order below the signal price and waits for a pullback) with the adverse delayed entry (Buy/Sell Stop, a long places a stop order above the signal price and waits for a breakout). Almost all of the gain comes from the favorable delayed entry: shallower drawdowns and higher profit factors, paid for with fewer filled signals.
  • Layer 2: trend filter, Which SuperTrend filter, in which volatility?: skips a day when the SuperTrend opposes the trade, checked against circular shifts of the skipped days to rule out luck. The rule picks the 1-hour SuperTrend, vetoing longs only when daily volatility is high and shorts only when it is low.
  • Layer 3: filter attribution, Where is the information in a trend filter?: looks at the veto and the delayed entry together. For longs the information is in the SuperTrend, for shorts mostly in the low-volatility state; per-setup filter picks do not carry over, so one rule is kept for every setup and the parents for the stop-loss layer are chosen.
  • Layer 4: SL/TP, Where to put the stop and the target?: the stop is set from each parent’s own MAE, beyond the bulk of the trades, and kept only on a parameter plateau. Every robust stop makes the drawdown shallower; a take profit is almost never kept.
  • Layer 5: breakeven stop, Where should breakeven trigger?: the trigger sits only where losing trades rarely reach. Few setups keep one, and only G09 short at 06:30 improves clearly.

How these nine setups trade together, with correlation, spread and slippage, and position size on an FTMO account, is in the series portfolio article, Portfolio and position size.

The Monday Market Profile starts at the same time on the previous Friday and covers Friday’s session and the Sunday evening session, consistent with the other days. Monday trades still differ: weekend news can open Monday farther from the previous value area than on other days, and the root study already found that an unconditional long at these times does unusually well on Mondays.

The 06:30 shorts lose on Mondays at every stage, with or without the veto; Monday’s long drift works against them. G05 short at 08:30 is the exception and does better on Mondays. For G08 long at 09:30 the raw signal makes almost all of its money on Mondays; the stop loss is what makes Tuesday–Friday profitable. The two 10:30 G13 setups have too few Monday trades to judge and are left out.

Every layer picked its parameters on the full Monday-to-Friday sample, but Mondays are only a small part of it, so the Tuesday-to-Friday results outweigh them, and the Monday difference does not go away just because Mondays are in the sample. In live trading some setups could skip Mondays, but that decision was made after seeing the results and needs confirmation on future data.

Are the mirror events better?

G13 (one or two bars above VAH, then back below) and G15 (a break above the POC that fails) are events the root study added for the mirror test, mirroring G04 below VAL and G07, the reclaim of the POC. Their final strategies have the highest profit factors in the set, but the three G13 longs have only a few dozen to about a hundred trades, and G15, the one large mirror setup, is the weakest final: the largest drawdown, −8.5 vol−17.5%.

Read with caution:Each stage was chosen on the same 2022–2026 history, so the final results are in-sample. Costs are not included. G13 N1 long at 10:30 and G13 N2 long at 10:30 have few trades. This is not trading advice.

What does this mean?

Layer by layer, the series turned nine raw Market Profile signals into strategies with much shallower drawdowns, mostly through the stop loss and, for shorts, the volatility-aware veto. Whether they hold up after spread and slippage, and how large each position can be on a prop-firm account, is the subject of the series portfolio article, Portfolio and position size.

Questions this study answers

How do the final strategies improve on the raw signals, and which step contributes most?

All nine final strategies have a shallower drawdown and a higher recovery factor than their raw signal, and the stop loss contributes most: G08 long at 09:30 goes from a max drawdown of −10.6 vol−17.6% to −5.4 vol−12.2% with the stop. G09 short at 06:30 goes from a recovery factor of 0.06−0.14 as a raw signal to 9.817.18 as a final strategy.

Is the SuperTrend × volatility veto still needed at the last stage?

Yes, especially for shorts. The veto raises the profit factor of all nine and the recovery factor of seven, and the two 06:30 shorts gain the most: G15 short at 06:30 ends with a recovery factor of 0.580.06 without the veto and 2.341.30 with it.

Can these strategies be traded on Mondays?

It depends on the direction and time. The 06:30 shorts lose on Mondays at every stage: G09 short’s final strategy has a profit factor of 0.910.89 on Mondays and 1.691.58 on Tuesday–Friday. G05 short at 08:30 does better on Mondays.

Questions raised along the way

Each layer picked its parameters separately. Do they still hold in one backtest?

The final parameters are generated from each layer’s outputs, not picked from one large search. This article reruns the nine setups in a single backtest, stage by stage, so each step is measured on its own: the veto alone is mixed, the delayed entry mainly removes trades, and the stop loss cuts the drawdown most.

The Monday profile is now consistent with other days. Why are Mondays still different?

The Monday profile starts at the same time on the previous Friday and covers Friday’s session and the Sunday evening session; but weekend news can open Monday far from the previous value area, and Mondays are only a small part of the selection sample, outweighed by Tuesday to Friday. The study splits each final strategy’s trades into Mondays and Tuesday–Friday; skipping Mondays is a choice made after seeing the results and needs confirmation on future data.

The mirror events have the highest final profit factors. Should they come first?

Not yet. The three G13 longs have only a few dozen to about a hundred trades, and G15 short at 06:30, the one large mirror setup, is the weakest final strategy, with the largest drawdown, −8.5 vol−17.5%.
