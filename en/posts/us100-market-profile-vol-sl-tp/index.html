Where to put the stop and the target? SL/TP on 21 US100 Market Profile parents

This is layer 4 of the Market Profile signals series: stop loss and take profit. The previous article is layer 3, Where is the information in a trend filter?, which left 21 parents: Market Profile signals from the root study with a delayed entry and a SuperTrend × volatility veto that beats random timing, every trade held to 16:00. This layer adds only a stop loss (SL) and a take profit (TP), keeping the entry and the veto. G01–G16 are Market Profile event codes (for example, G08 = a whole bar closes above the POC); the glossary lists every condition, and SL and TP are multiples of the daily ATR at entry (volatility mode). The question is where a stop loss and a take profit belong. Both are set from the parent’s own excursions: the stop from where winning trades go against you, the target from how far winners run. A setting is kept only if its neighbours are as good, so a lucky single value does not pass. Everything is measured in volatility mode.

Conclusion

Nine of the 21 parents get a robust stop; only one also keeps a take profit. Every robust stop lowers the max drawdown and raises the recovery factor, and for G09 short at 06:30 and G08 long at 09:30 it also raises the total clearly. For the other 12 parents no stop passes the plateau rule, so they keep holding to 16:00.

How are the stop and the target chosen?

The stop is searched where winning trades rarely go: from the midpoint of the winners’ MAE Q2 and Q3 to all trades’ MAE Q3. A tighter stop would cut trades that usually end as winners. The target is tried at the winners’ G_MFE Q1, Q2 and Q3, plus no target. Each pair is scored on its trades (profit factor, Sortino, win rate) and on what the parent’s own path implies a stop of that size would earn per unit of risk.

A pair is robust only if it scores above zero, makes money in both 2022–2024 and 2025–2026, puts the stop at or above the parent’s median MAE (so at least half of the parent’s trades never reach it), and every stop within ±0.04 at the same target also beats the parent on both recovery factor and max drawdown. Among robust pairs the highest score is kept.

Why is the take profit rarely kept?

Only G04 N1 long at 08:30 keeps a target (0.38 with a 0.30 stop). Many target settings beat the parent too, but the stop-only version usually scores higher: these intraday trades already end at 16:00, and a target mostly cuts the few days that run far. The stop, not the target, is what reshapes the result.

What happens to the other 12 parents?

For 12 parents, including G11 long at 09:30 and 10:30 and G03 long at 10:30, no stop passes the plateau rule: a good recovery appears at one stop but not at its neighbours, or the stop needed sits inside the trade cluster. They keep holding to 16:00. The parent with the deepest drawdown, G15 short at 06:30, is rescued: its recovery factor goes from 0.620.20 to 2.131.23.

Read with caution:Each stop was picked after looking at the data from a grid of up to about 100 settings per parent; the plateau rule limits but does not remove that bias. Costs are not included, and stop exits pay slippage. This is not trading advice.

What does this mean?

A stop set from the parent’s own MAE, above the bulk of the trades, helped where it was robust: lower drawdown on every kept setup and higher return on most. Targets add little to trades that already close at 16:00. The nine robust setups go on to layer 5: the breakeven stop.

Questions this study answers

Do a stop and a target set from each parent's own MAE and G_MFE improve the 21 parents?

Nine get a robust stop, and each lowers the max drawdown and raises the recovery factor: G08 long at 09:30 with a 0.38 stop goes from −10.6 vol−17.6% to −5.4 vol−12.2%. For the other 12 no stop passes the plateau rule, and they keep holding to 16:00.

Do these intraday trades need a take profit?

Rarely: of the nine robust picks only G04 N1 long at 08:30 keeps a target (0.38 with a 0.30 stop). These trades already close at 16:00, so a target mostly cuts the few days that run far; the stop is what reshapes the result.

Questions raised along the way

How tight can the stop be without cutting trades that end as winners?

The search starts at the midpoint of the winners’ MAE Q2 and Q3 (the 50th and 75th percentiles) and runs to all trades’ MAE Q3; the stop must also sit at or above the parent’s median MAE, so at least half of the parent’s trades never reach it.

With about 100 settings per parent, how is a lucky single stop avoided?

By requiring a plateau: every stop within ±0.04 at the same target must beat the parent on both recovery factor and max drawdown, and the pair must make money in both 2022–2024 and 2025–2026. This limits the selection bias but does not remove it.

The stop lowers the win rate. Why does the total rise?

The stop ends a few deep losers early. G09 short at 06:30 with a 0.30 stop drops its win rate from 47.3%47.3% to 39.5%39.5%, while its total rises from +13.5 vol+13.0% to +25.2 vol+35.3%.

Research setup
Article type
Parameter sweep
Instrument
US100.cash
Direction
Both directions
Bars
US100 (FTMO) 15-minute execution, New York time; completed bars only
Parents
The 21 setups of the filter layer whose matched SuperTrend x volatility veto beats more than half of its circular shifts and whose expectancy is positive in 2022–2024 and 2025–2026; entry and veto unchanged, held to 16:00
Unit
SL, TP and the delayed-entry distance are multiples of the previous completed New York daily ATR (72 daily bars, about 60 trading days) at entry
SL range
Every 0.02 from the winners' MAE (Q2 + Q3) / 2 to all trades' MAE Q3 of the parent, plus none
TP range
The winners' G_MFE Q1, Q2 and Q3 of the parent, plus none (hold to 16:00)
Score
PF^0.4 × Sortino^0.4 × win rate^0.2 of the candidate, times the parent-implied expectancy per unit of stop; each SL averaged with its ±0.02 neighbours; 10th percentile of 2,000 day bootstraps
Robust
Score above 0; expectancy positive in 2022–2024 and 2025–2026; SL at or above the parent's median MAE; every SL within ±0.04 at the same TP beats the parent on both recovery factor and max drawdown
Sample
New York entries from late January 2022 to September 23, 2026, Monday to Friday, on the days the veto keeps
Costs
Not included
Parameters
Signal: 13 values, full list in the strategy report; Direction: Long / Short; Entry time: 06:30 / 08:30 / 09:30 / 10:30; Delayed entry (daily ATR): 11 values, full list in the strategy report; Stop loss SL (daily ATR): 26 values, full list in the strategy report; Take profit TP (daily ATR): 33 values, full list in the strategy report
Return calculation
Volatility-normalized: Sum of per-trade P&L ÷ volatility at exit (exit_volatility); Percentage: Sum of per-trade returns (simple)
