Do popular indicator signals have an edge? 34 default-parameter signals on NAS100: path asymmetry and delayed limit orders, 2018–2025

The signals taught by technical-analysis textbooks, built into TradingView and MetaTrader, and explained on broker tutorial pages are the signals most people see at the same time. This research does not ask whether they are “right”; it asks two testable questions written down before any data was read: in the four hours after a signal, does the price path show a tradeable asymmetry (an overshoot first, or a stop-loss sweep before the continuation)? And do delayed limit orders placed around the signal earn anything before costs? Thirty-four signals, two instruments, two timeframes, all at platform defaults, each test with its own control and a false-discovery-rate (FDR) threshold inside its family. Finally the engine backtest of simply holding every signal for 4 hours is laid out and compared with the market over the same calendar. Unless the page mode is switched, every return is in volatility mode: each trade’s P&L ÷ the 72-day mean daily True Range at exit; see the research glossary.

Conclusion

No. After a level-type signal (a close outside the Bollinger bands, a Donchian breakout, the prior-day high or low, the opening range, a round number, CCI ±100, a close through SMA 20, a SuperTrend flip) the path over the next 2 to 6 hours is wider than 1 to 4 hours later, but it widens on the favorable and the adverse side alike, the order of the extremes shows no asymmetry, and the excess on offer is single digits to the low teens in index points; after a moving-average cross there is nothing at all. Delayed limit orders around these signals, fading them or joining after the sweep: none of 2,304 combinations passes the family FDR threshold, and the expectancy per fill is negative before any cost. Holding each of the 34 signals for 16 fifteen-minute bars, 75 of 136 strategies have a positive total, and against the day-hold market of the same side at the same share of time in the market, 72 earn more per hour than the market, exactly half.

  1. Of 136 strategies (34 signals × 2 instruments × 2 timeframes) held for 4 hours, 75 have a positive total return: 53 long and 22 short; the mean expectancy per trade is +0.011 vol for the longs and −0.004 vol for the shorts. Evidence: Returns
  2. Only 2 strategies are positive in every year; the longs are positive in 64% of their years on average, the shorts in 40%. Evidence: Consistency over time
  3. The mean max drawdown of the 136 strategies is −13.2 vol; on the common calendar from 2022-06, only 4 strategies have a max drawdown below the same-side market’s max drawdown times their share of time in the market. Evidence: Risk

How was the study done?

Data and signals. The 15-minute bars are the stored bars; the engine resamples the 1-hour and 30-minute bars from them on the New York wall clock, every 1-hour bucket holding exactly 4 and every 30-minute bucket exactly 2 fifteen-minute bars, and computes the indicators at their defaults on the signal timeframe. The selection period runs from 2018 (NAS100) or 2022-01-24 (US100: 15-minute bars exist from 2021-10-31 and the volatility unit from 2022-01-24) to 2025-12-31. The whole of 2026 is the validation stretch: never read, data fetched only to 2026-01-01.

Unit. The volatility scale at each signal bar is the simple mean of the True Range of the previous 72 completed New York daily bars, frozen at the signal bar; paths, order distances, expectancies and returns are all divided by it.

Stage 1: the path. From the first 15-minute bar after the signal bar, K = 16 bars (4 hours; 8 and 24 checked for the same sign) give three quantities: the favorable excursion before the adverse extreme (MFE, the largest favorable distance before the adverse extreme), the maximum adverse excursion (MAE), the global favorable excursion (G_MFE), plus the share of signals whose favorable extreme comes before the adverse one. Each quantity’s median (Q2) is read as an excess over two controls: the forward-shift control moves the same signals 4, 8 and 16 bars later and asks whether the path right after the signal differs from the path 1 to 4 hours later (same day, same volatility state); the random-timestamp control draws as many timestamps at the same weekday and hour. The excess divided by the standard error of a trading-day bootstrap (200 draws) is the t. Power: the noise ceiling of 36 candidates, √(2 ln 36) ≈ 2.7, the smallest excess worth trading δ = 0.05 units, and the per-signal standard deviation measured on random timestamps give the required counts, 28 / 58 / 86 signals at K = 8 / 16 / 24; only 2 of the 136 signal × instrument × timeframe cells fall short (US100 1-hour SMA 50/200 long and short at K = 24). A family is instrument × timeframe × K, controlled at Benjamini–Hochberg q ≤ 0.10. Robustness flags: the same sign on the three K, on both instruments and in every year with at least 20 signals (the MFE Q2 excess), and FDR passed in all six cells (2 instruments × 3 K).

Stage 2: delayed limit orders. The signal is only a reference point; the entry is a delayed limit order, one opportunity per signal, no trade when it does not fill (intention to treat, never re-placed). Two hypotheses: the fade places a limit order against the signal at a distance d in the signal’s direction from the signal close, d the parent’s MFE Q2 or Q3, with the target at the parent’s MAE Q2 beyond the signal close on the other side; the join after the sweep places a limit order with the signal at d′ against it, d′ the parent’s MAE Q2 or Q3, with the target at the parent’s MFE Q2 beyond the signal close in the signal’s direction. The order is valid for 16 fifteen-minute bars, the time exit comes 16 bars after the fill, there is no stop, and the MAE after the fill is reported. The parent quantiles come in two versions: rolling (6-month lookback, refreshed quarterly) and whole-history (in-sample reference). Volatility conditions use only what is known at the signal: the signal bar’s True Range ÷ unit at or above that signal’s median, or the mean True Range of the 8 fifteen-minute bars before the signal ÷ unit at or above its median; unconditional and both conditions were each run. Every control is an engine Run with the same order structure: the flags shifted 4, 8 and 16 bars later; flags drawn at random per row at the signal’s historical weekday × hour rate; and the mirror structure (the fade’s distance placed with the signal). The signal set is the 8 signals that passed every Stage 1 flag plus both sides of the other level-type families, 16 per timeframe; the moving-average families do not enter Stage 2. One row per signal × hypothesis × quantile × condition × instrument × quantile source, 2,304 rows, Benjamini–Hochberg q ≤ 0.10 inside each family (instrument × timeframe × quantile source). The engine Runs: 90 quarterly Runs plus 4 whole-history reference Runs.

The article’s trades. The strategy report on this page is a separate set of engine Runs: one strategy per signal, entering at the open of the first 15-minute bar after the signal bar and exiting at the open 16 bars later, no stop loss or take profit. The engine holds one position per strategy, so a signal within 16 bars after the previous one is skipped and the trade counts are below the Stage 1 signal counts (round numbers and the close through SMA 20 are skipped most). The Ichimoku and Donchian windows count 15-minute bars, so a signal next to a data gap can differ by one bar from a bar count (a sample of 10 signals over 9 months: 1 trade each). The market comparison reads the day-hold market strategy of the same instrument and side over the market benchmarks’ common calendar (from 2022-06-01) in three ways: the rate per hour, the strategy’s total against the market’s total × the strategy’s share of time in the market, and the strategy’s max drawdown against the market’s.

Stage 1: what is different about the path after a signal?

Four things, NAS100 1-hour K = 16 as the example; US100 and the 30-minute signals look the same:

  1. After a level-type signal the whole path widens, on both sides, and the widening decays with time. Bollinger bands, Donchian breakouts, the prior-day high and low, the opening range, round numbers, CCI ±100, the close through SMA 20, SuperTrend flips: MFE Q2 excess +0.01 to +0.025 units (t 2.5 to 5.6), MAE Q2 excess +0.01 to +0.05 (t 2.4 to 6.0), G_MFE Q2 excess +0.02 to +0.05 (t 3 to 5.4). For example the Bollinger short’s MFE Q2 is 0.073 against 0.055 for the forward-shift control, its MAE 0.221 against 0.183, its G_MFE 0.223 against 0.182; the opening-range short’s MAE is 0.223 against 0.176. This is a burst of volatility in the hours after the signal, not a one-sided overshoot: the favorable and the adverse side grow together.
  2. The order of the extremes shows no asymmetry. The share of signals whose favorable extreme comes first is 0.45 to 0.54, against 0.45 to 0.53 for the forward-shift control and 0.47 to 0.53 for the random-timestamp control; the t of the order is almost always within ±2. The claim “overshoot first, then the stop sweep” has no support; the median bar of both extremes is the 6th to 9th (1.5 to 2.5 hours).
  3. After a moving-average cross there is nothing. EMA 9/21, SMA 20/50, SMA 50/200, the MACD signal line, Ichimoku: the t of MFE, MAE and G_MFE is mostly within ±2 and the excess at most 0.01 units.
  4. Size. The largest MFE Q2 excess is about +0.025 units, 6 to 10 index points at a NAS100 daily True Range of 250 to 400 points; the MAE excess reaches +0.05, 12 to 20 points. Short signals show larger excesses than longs throughout (NAS100 rose over the eight years, and downside breaks happen in high-volatility sessions).

The signals that pass every robustness flag, 1-hour: SuperTrend 10/3 flip · short, Bollinger 20/2 close outside the bands · short, Opening 30-minute range breakout · long, Round number (every 100 points) · long, Round number (every 100 points) · short; 30-minute: Donchian 20 breakout · short, Round number (every 100 points) · long, Round number (every 100 points) · short. Most other level-type signals fail the yearly sign flag: the size of the volatility burst is unstable from year to year. This list holds candidates for “a wider path after the signal than 1 to 4 hours later”, not candidates for fading.

What does this mean?

The counterparty hypothesis predicted an overshoot pushed by signal chasers, then a pullback that sweeps the early stops, then the continuation. What was measured: after a level-type signal the path widens on both sides for 2 to 6 hours and the widening decays, the order shows no asymmetry; after a moving-average cross there is no path difference; the tradeable overshoot is a few points to the low teens. Stop-loss clustering explains why levels (the prior-day high and low, round numbers, range edges) react, but the data shows no one-sided overshoot before the cluster is swept. Under the delayed limit-order structure, neither entry hypothesis has an expectancy that can be told apart from its controls, and the expectancy after the fill is negative before cost; the volatility conditions, the quantile version and the instrument change nothing. Holding the signals for 4 hours beats the same-side market exactly half the time, like a coin.

Not done here, and not recommended under the same hypothesis: cost, correlation, combination, profit estimates, stop-loss grids, grids over the distance or the target (that would be a search for extremes inside the controls). 2026 was never read.

Before you read on:

Returns are the simple sum of per-trade results, with no position size or compounding; the unit follows the page mode, by default each trade’s P&L ÷ the 72-day mean daily True Range at exit. The selection period is 2018 (US100: 2022) to 2025, all in-sample readings; the whole of 2026 is the validation stretch and was not read. NAS100 and US100 are the same index at two brokers, not independent evidence. The 136 strategies are not independent attempts: the long and short side of a family, and the same signal on two timeframes, are highly correlated. The trades in the strategy report carry no spread, commission or swap. This is a backtest on CFD bars, not a trading record, and not investment advice.

Questions this study answers

After a popular signal at platform-default parameters, is the price path asymmetric in a tradeable way?

After a level-type signal the favorable and the adverse side of the path widen together for 2 to 6 hours and the widening decays; the share of signals whose favorable extreme comes first equals the controls’. After a moving-average cross there is no difference at all. Only the 1-hour SuperTrend short, Bollinger lower-band short, opening-range long and round-number long and short, and the 30-minute Donchian short and round-number long and short pass every robustness flag, and they are candidates for “a wider path”, not for fading.

Do delayed limit orders around the signal, fading it or joining after the sweep, have an expectancy before cost?

No. None of the 2,304 combinations (signal × hypothesis × quantile × volatility condition × instrument × quantile source) passes the family FDR threshold; the mean expectancy per fill is negative, and so is the mean excess over the forward-shift and random-timestamp controls; the volatility conditions only halve the fill rate.

Does simply holding every signal for 4 hours beat the market of the same side?

75 of 136 strategies have a positive total, but at the same share of time in the market only 72 earn more per hour than the same-side day-hold market, exactly half; only 4 have a max drawdown below the market × share.

Questions raised along the way

Why does the shift control move the signals later only, never earlier?

A window shifted earlier starts before the signal bar and contains the move that produced the signal (the decline before a Bollinger short, for example), so it measures the signal’s definition rather than the path after it. Windows shifted 4, 8 and 16 bars later lie after the signal and exclude its move, on the same day and in the same volatility state, and ask the question that matters: is the path right after the signal different from the path 1 to 4 hours later? The random-timestamp control is the unconditional reference, but its unit is the 72-day mean True Range, which does not follow the volatility of the moment, and signals cluster in high-volatility periods, so it carries both the signal and the volatility state the signal occurs in.

Does re-selecting the signals with the widest path each period carry any time-varying information?

A fixed universe, monthly steps, a 3- or 6-month lookback, a 3- or 6-month hold, the top 6 selected: the Spearman correlation between lookback and holding scores averages 0.12 to 0.21 with t 3.2 to 4.7, a random pick of 6 gives about 0, and there is no decay. But after each signal’s own full-period mean is removed the correlation is about 0 (−0.04 to +0.01): all of it comes from stable differences between signals (the round-number, Donchian and Bollinger shorts are in the top 6 almost every period), the period-to-period changes are unpredictable, and the whole-history table already holds everything the procedure can give.

Why does the strategy report hold fewer trades than Stage 1 holds signals?

The engine keeps one position per strategy, and so that every trade holds exactly 16 bars, a signal within 16 bars after the previous one is skipped; round numbers and the close through SMA 20 can fire every hour and are skipped most (NAS100 1-hour round-number long: 5,154 Stage 1 signals, 2,993 trades in the report). The Stage 1 path statistics use every signal and do not depend on the trade count.

Why are the Parabolic SAR and the MACD histogram not included?

The Parabolic SAR is a bar-by-bar recursive state that the engine’s indicator catalog does not hold and its expressions cannot express, so it is not tested. The MACD histogram is the MACD line minus the signal line, so its zero-line cross is family 6’s signal-line cross; family 7 therefore uses the MACD line crossing zero.
