Shorting NAS100 at the open on option expiration day: the one survivor of 45 pre-registered hypotheses

Most technical-indicator strategies do not survive a statistical test, so this research started from the other end: from the people who have to trade at a known time regardless of price. Forty-five hypotheses about such forced flows were written down before any data was read, each with its own control and a false-discovery-rate threshold inside its family. This post follows the one that held, a one-hour short on NAS100 at the open of monthly option expiration day, through the base reading, the entry and exit layers, cost, sizing and the single read of 2026. Unless the page mode is switched, every number is in volatility mode: each trade’s P&L ÷ the daily median True Range at exit; see the research glossary.

Conclusion

Selling NAS100 at 09:30 New York on monthly option expiration day and buying back at 10:30 earned +10.7 vol+15.3%+$6,268 over 100100100 signals on Pepperstone since 2018, with a profit factor of 2.042.031.75; on FTMO US100 since 2022 the same rule earned +6.4 vol+9.7%+$4,101 over 545454 signals. The edge sits in the first bars after the open: every delayed entry tested (resting Sell Stop or Sell Limit orders at increasing distances) lowered the total, and none of six exit layers cut the drawdown on both brokers by the pre-registered rule. The frozen node keeps the smallest Sell Stop, a wide stop for sizing and a small take profit; it trades once a month and its net margin after cost is thin.

Why option expiration day, and why the open?

On the third Friday of each month the monthly index options expire. Dealers who are short gamma into expiration unwind the hedges that pinned prices during the week, and most of that unwinding happens around the cash open, when the options’ delta collapses. That gives a time at which a known group has to trade regardless of price, which is the kind of signal that survived in two rounds of pre-registered hypotheses while closing-auction rebalancing, month-end flows, the FOMC cycle, earnings nights, gold and FX fixing times and index rebalance days did not. The short direction follows the reading of the first round: on the same days, holding short through the whole session is not better than any other day; only the first hour is.

Does the base signal hold on both brokers?

The stage-0 rule has no parameter to choose: sell at the 09:30 open, buy back at 10:30, every monthly expiration day. In the first round its excess over the same hour on all other days, and over the same hour on the other Fridays, cleared the within-weekday permutation null on NAS100, US500 and FTMO US100; US30 did not clear it and US2000 was borderline, so this post keeps the two Nasdaq contracts.

Does a resting Sell Stop improve the entry?

Delayed entries were tested as intention to treat: an unfilled signal counts as zero, so the comparison is on the base signal’s full set of days. Two families were tried: Sell Stops below the open at one to five times the winners’ MAE Q1 (favorable entries), and Sell Limits above the open at one to five times the losers’ G_MFE Q3 (adverse entries). Every distance lowered the total on NAS100 and the pattern was the same on US500 and US2000: the further the fill is from the open, the less is left. The research rule adopted the smallest favorable distance when none passed, so the frozen node enters on a Sell Stop 0.025 units below the 09:30 open.The stop fills on 848484 of 100100100 signals on NAS100; the missed ones were mostly winners that fell straight away.

Do a stop and a take profit change the path?

Six exit layers were tested on the Sell Stop entry, one at a time, each judged by the whole-sample max drawdown against its parent with a 200-draw session bootstrap and a plateau requirement, on NAS100 and US500 together: a stop loss at the parent’s MAE Q75 to Q95, a take profit at the G_MFE and at the MFE quantiles, a breakeven stop at both quantile sets, and a trailing stop. None passed on both instruments. Two were then set by the researcher’s disposition: the widest stop (MAE Q95, 0.73 units) because a position size needs a stop, and the take profit at the median non-zero MFE (0.165 units), which passed on NAS100 and US2000 and missed the bootstrap threshold on US500 by 0.04.

What does it earn after cost, and how big can it be?

Switch the page to USD mode for the sizing used in this study: USD 750 per full stop-out, lots = 750 ÷ (0.73 × the daily median True Range at entry), floored to 0.1 lot, 4 points of round-trip cost per trade. At that size the frozen node earns +0.087 vol+12.89 bps+$65.475 per trade in volatility units, about one trade a month. The margin after cost is thin: the take profit is 0.165 units, about 30 to 40 index points at 2025 volatility, against 4 points of cost, so the 90% interval of the net mean per trade touches zero in the internal reading. The base of USD 750 was chosen so that the 95th percentile of the bootstrap drawdown stays inside USD 3,600, an FTMO 100K daily limit; a USD 8,000 budget allows about USD 1,660 per stop-out.

Read with care:

Returns are the simple sum of per-trade results, without compounding; the unit follows the page mode. The selection period is 2018 to 2025 and the parameters were chosen on it; 2026 was read once after the freeze on 2026-10-28 and is included in the tables. One trade a month gives 848484 filled trades in nine years on NAS100, so every statistic has a wide interval, and the 2018–2019 years were weak. The Sell Stop entry and the two exit settings did not beat the base signal by the pre-registered drawdown rule; they are the researcher’s disposition, recorded as such. This is a backtest on CFD bars, not a trading record, and not investment advice.

Questions this study answers

Of the structural-flow hypotheses, which ones survive their own control and the family's false-discovery threshold?

Only the option-expiration short did, at the family level, in both rounds: its excess over the same hour on other days and over the other Fridays cleared the permutation null on NAS100, US500 and FTMO US100. Closing rebalancing, month-end flows, the FOMC cycle, earnings nights, gold and FX fixing times, index rebalance days and the conditioning of the pre-FOMC drift on volatility all stayed inside their nulls or were undecidable at the sample size.

Does the option-expiration short hold on both brokers and across years?

On Pepperstone NAS100 the base signal was positive in 887 of 999 years with a max drawdown of −1.3 vol−1.5%−$1,219; on FTMO US100 in 555 of 555. 2018 and 2019 were the weak years.

Do entry and exit layers add anything on top of the base signal?

No layer passed the pre-registered rule on both instruments. Delayed entries lowered the total at every distance because the move starts at the open; the take profit lowered the NAS100 drawdown and raised the win rate but fell short of the bootstrap threshold on US500; the stop is kept for sizing.

Questions raised along the way

Is the edge the day or the hour?

The hour. Holding short from 09:30 to the session close on the same days earns a lower rate per hour, and holding short through the whole session on expiration days is not better than on other days. The dealer hedging that motivates the signal is concentrated at the open.

Could a filter remove a losing class of expiration days?

Twenty-one states known at 09:30 were tried as filters, each with a circular-shift null: the previous day’s direction, the opening gap, the five- and twenty-day direction, realized volatility, the overnight range, the position of the open against the prior value area, the prior session’s initial balance, and daily SuperTrend settings. None passed on both instruments. Every split removed a half that was still profitable; the only consistent direction was that the short worked better when the daily SuperTrend was bullish, the opposite of a trend veto, and it is kept on watch.

Is the 143-day volatility window too slow?

Windows of 20, 60 and 143 days gave the same fills, the same exits and nearly the same result in points; the window only changes the position size. The 143-day window looked best on NAS100 because it did not shrink size in 2020, which is luck rather than merit; 60 days looked best on FTMO; neither won on both brokers, so the node keeps 143 days.

What did 2026 say when read once after the freeze?

Ten expiration days. The year grid above shows 2026 on both brokers: on NAS100 the node’s 2026 sits in the upper half of the band that the selection years would predict for ten signals; on FTMO US100 it is positive as well. US500, the sibling that is not in this post, ended 2026 below its band and stays on watch only.
