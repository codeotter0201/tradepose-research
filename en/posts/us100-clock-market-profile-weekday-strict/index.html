This article redoes it on the same data under a stricter standard: shorts must beat holding short, the random-day null is raised, every strategy must hold up both with and against the trend, and exploration ignores costs and correlation entirely until every path is finished. Unless the page is switched, every number is in volatility mode: each trade’s P&L divided by the daily median True Range at exit (the median of each day’s True Range over the previous 143 New York sessions), and every stop, target and delayed-entry distance is a multiple of it; see the research glossary.

Conclusion

41 roots (weekday × clock window × Market Profile gate) grew into 131 finished strategies. 84 of them beat holding the same side in the full sample and in both halves and hold up in both trend regimes; they come from 19 roots. After costs, 6 Tuesday shorts have no edge left. Of the rest, branches of the same window that differ only in their exits all correlate at 0.5 or more, and the Monday long windows overlap heavily, so 6 strategies remain: four Monday longs, a Tuesday short and a Thursday long. What changed from the earlier article?

Four steps of the earlier selection are tightened here; every other rule is the same:

  1. Shorts are compared with the same side. The earlier article compared every cell with holding long through the same window. Holding long through a short window loses money anyway, so shorts won easily. Here longs are compared with holding long and shorts with holding short, so a cell without a Market Profile gate is the market itself and never passes: every passing cell carries an edge that comes from its gate.
  2. The null level is calibrated. Each random-day draw takes the best window of the whole family (side × weekday × gate) as the benchmark, on the t-statistic. Of 217 families, 25 pass at the 90% level against at most 21.7 by chance; 15 against 10.9 at 95%; 11 against 2.2 at 99%, all of them Monday longs and Tuesday shorts. This article uses 90%: not strong evidence, but much cleaner than a 50% level, without shrinking to two combinations.
  3. Exploration ignores costs and correlation. The earlier article merged highly correlated plateaus while picking roots. Here every plateau is a root, and costs, correlation and the combination all come at the end.
  4. Both trend regimes must hold. The US100 move over the previous 60 sessions splits every trade into with-trend and against-trend. A strategy fails when, in either regime, its profit factor is below 1 and a random draw of the same number of its own trades is that weak at most 5% of the time: the weakness belongs to the regime, not to the noise of a small subset.

Where is the edge?

22,693 cells beat holding the same side; 356 of them pass the null and form 41 plateaus, the 41 roots: 28 Monday longs, 9 Tuesday shorts, and 2 each of Thursday longs and Thursday shorts. Wednesday and Friday have none. As in the earlier article, the evidence sits in Monday longs and Tuesday shorts.

What did the entry and exit layers change?

The 41 roots grew 131 finished strategies, 86 of them robust; 2 of those fail a trend regime: Monday long 19:30→14:00 has a profit factor of only 0.63 against the trend, and Tuesday short 18:00→19:00 only 0.55 with it. How large a position can an FTMO 100K account take?

Sizing follows FTMO account position sizing and the earlier article: lots come from the stop distance, so a stop-out loses a fixed amount; each weekday is a group whose multiplier is half a risk-parity estimate and half equal; the worst case of every strategy of a day hitting its stop, and the backtest drawdown, are checked against the daily USD 4,500 and total USD 10,000 limits, each used only to 80%.

The result is Monday 0.86, Tuesday 1.17 and Thursday 0.97 on a base of USD 983: a stop-out costs USD 845 on Monday, 1,150 on Tuesday and 954 on Thursday. Lots = that amount ÷ (stop multiple × the daily median True Range at entry), floored to 0.01 lot. Over the whole sample, net of 4 points per trade, the combination makes USD 111,223 with a max drawdown of 4,288 and a recovery factor of 25.9; the worst day loses 2,497, and a day with every stop hit costs about 3,600.

For an honest test, the multipliers and the base are set on 2022–2024 only and judged on 2025–2026:

The adopted design clearly beats the history-only rules: about the same profit with a drawdown a quarter smaller. This time, though, equal stop-outs have the higher recovery factor: Tuesday and Thursday hold one strategy each, risk parity gives these single strategies more risk, and the drawdown grows with it. Over the full sample the two are almost the same (recovery factor 25.9 against 26.2). The design was set before these results were seen, so it stays; if the Tuesday and Thursday multipliers start swinging at the quarterly re-estimate, it will be reviewed.

Before trading:Strategies with close stops trade large lots (Monday long 18:00→19:30, the Tuesday short and the Thursday long: about 18–20 lots at typical volatility), so check the broker’s lot and margin limits before ordering. The daily-limit check uses stop-out amounts; FTMO also counts intraday floating losses. The roots were picked on the full sample; out-of-sample confirmation covers only the entry and exit layers and the sizing multipliers. This is not trading advice.

What does this mean?

Under the stricter standard, only Monday longs, Tuesday shorts and one Thursday long keep a weekday × Market Profile edge, and the many Monday windows are really one edge. The final 6 are fewer than the earlier article’s 14, but each passed the same-side comparison, the null, both trend regimes and costs.

Questions this study answers

Which weekday × clock window × Market Profile combinations survive a stricter standard?

All 41 roots are Monday longs, Tuesday shorts and Thursday longs and shorts; the 84 robust finished strategies come from Monday long, Tuesday short and one Thursday long root. The final 6 are four Monday longs, a Tuesday short and a Thursday long.

How many remain after costs and correlation?

78 have room after costs, the 6 dropped all being Tuesday shorts; branches of the same window correlate highly and the Monday long windows overlap heavily, so 6 remain with correlations below 0.5, at most 0.47.

How large a position can an FTMO 100K account take with the 6 together?

A stop-out costs USD 983 × the weekday multiplier (Monday 0.86, Tuesday 1.17, Thursday 0.97), and every stop of a day hit together stays within 80% of the daily limit. With the multipliers and base set on 2022–2024 only, 2025–2026 makes USD 63,695 with a max drawdown of USD 3,946.

Questions raised along the way

Could the windows trade every session instead of one weekday?

The same strict scan was run on clock window × Market Profile gate trading every session, against holding the same side on every session. Across 48 side × gate families, the number of passing families matches chance at every level: 22 against 24 at 50%, 5 against 4.8 at 90%, 3 against 2.4 at 95%, and only one weak plateau forms. The Market Profile edge exists only on particular weekdays and is diluted by the other days when traded daily. So the weekday stays in the root, and weekdays are grouped again only for sizing.

Would sizing each trade from the strategy's own last 10 MAEs work better?

The platform’s default takes the 90th percentile of the last 10 MAEs (in volatility multiples) × the entry volatility × 4 as the expected loss in points. On these 6 it estimates 2.7–3.7 times the stop distance, so a real stop-out mostly costs only 0.25–0.56 of the risk amount but up to 1.34 times; the first 10 trades of each strategy get no order. The days with enlarged positions hit the daily limit first and force the whole book smaller: under the same two limits, 2025–2026 makes only USD 42,266 with a recovery factor of 18.1, both below stop-distance sizing. With hard stops, lots should come from the stop distance.

Should a VIX filter be added?

No. The VIX filter study found that shorting only while VIX trends up helps on raw clock windows, but the gain came mostly from 2022–2024 and did not carry over to finished strategies; sizing by VIX did not beat ATR sizing out of sample either.

Why did the exit ranking change from the earlier article?

The earlier exit tree broke ties between equal scores by the order of data rows, so a rerun on the same data could pick different exits. Here ties take the average rank and are then decided by grid position; two reruns give identical picks.

Roots
Clock windows on a 30-minute grid × one of 12 Market Profile gates (the entry time's two Profiles × open above / inside / below the value area, keep or skip) × Monday–Friday. A cell needs at least 70 trades and its mean result per trade must beat holding the same side through the same window on every session of that weekday (longs against holding long, shorts against holding short) over the full sample, 2022–2024 and 2025–2026, so a cell without a gate never passes; its t-statistic must beat the best window of the whole side × weekday × gate family in 90% of 1,000 random-day draws; passing cells form plateaus of at least two adjacent cells on the time grid, and every plateau's representative is a root
Layers
Delayed entry keeps the top 3 children per root; stop loss, take profit, breakeven and trailing stop keep the top 2 per parent; a later pick of the same kind sits more than 0.04 from an earlier one. Ranking: win rate, profit factor, recovery factor, mean result per trade and the layer's own measure; ties are broken by grid position, so a rerun gives the same picks
Delayed entry
Favorable delayed entry from 0.02 up to the winners' MAE Q1; adverse delayed entry from the losers' G_MFE Q2 − 0.06 to Q90 + 0.06; with no pass, favorable delayed entry 0.02
Stop loss
Mandatory; at or above the parent's MAE Q3 and at most MAE Q95; must lower the mean MAE; must sit outside the MAE cluster; with no pass, the closest stop to these rules
Take profit and breakeven
Must lower the mean MAE and not raise the max drawdown; breakeven triggers at or above the losers' G_MFE Q3
Trailing stop
Wider than the stop, must lower the mean MHL and sit outside the MHL cluster
Sample split
Every layer picks on 2022-06-20 to 2024 and keeps a pick only if 2025–2026 also beat the market and repeat the layer's effect
Robust
Finished strategies with at least 70 trades that beat holding the same side in full, in and out of sample; trend regime test: the US100 move over the previous 60 sessions marks each trade as with or against the trend, and a strategy fails when in either regime its profit factor is below 1 and a random draw of the same number of its own trades is that weak at most 5% of the time
Profit room
Net of 6 points per round trip (plus 9% a year swap on longs, Friday counting 3 nights) the mean result per trade stays positive in 2022–2024 and in 2025–2026
Correlation
Measured on the sessions both strategies traded (at least 20, otherwise 0); per root up to 3 with correlation below 0.5, then in rank order a strategy correlating 0.5 or more with a kept one is dropped
Sizing
FTMO 100K: total loss under USD 10,000, daily loss under 4,500. One group per weekday; group multiplier = half the risk-parity estimate from all history so far, half equal (1), kept within 0.8–1.25 and changed only when a new estimate moves more than 20%; equal stop-out USD inside a group; base risk is the largest amount keeping every stop of a day (each charged the sample's largest loss past a stop, plus cost) within 80% of 4,500 and the drawdown within 80% of 10,000

Volatility mode by default: each trade's P&L ÷ the daily median True Range (143 New York days) at exit; percentage and USD (the sizing above) are switchable
