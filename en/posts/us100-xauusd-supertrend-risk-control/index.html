A 1-hour SuperTrend as a risk switch: a five-strategy portfolio on US100 and gold

The SuperTrend is one of the most common trend indicators: it draws a band an ATR multiple away from price and flips when a close crosses it. This study started from “use a 1-hour SuperTrend for US100 entries and exits, with the VIX as a filter” and ended with a five-strategy portfolio across US100 and gold, intraday and held overnight. The SuperTrend’s job here is not to earn more but to lose less: for the same time in the market, the periods it picks have about half the drawdown of random periods. All figures are in volatility mode by default: each trade’s P&L divided by the daily ATR at entry, and stop, take-profit and delayed-entry distances are multiples of it; see the research glossary.

Conclusion

With one unit per strategy, the five made money in every year since March 2022: 165.7 ATR before costs with a maximum drawdown of 5.4; 125.7 and 5.9 after reference costs and swaps; and still 101.7 and 6.3 with costs 50% higher. Each strategy is safer than its market: the intraday ones had a lower drawdown than holding through the same window in every year, the held ones a lower drawdown than holding at all hours in every year. They trade different markets, sides or holding styles, and their daily correlations are between −0.16 and 0.08. Costs weigh most on the two gold intraday strategies, taking about 40% of their gain; they are thin on their own, but being almost uncorrelated with the rest they still raise the portfolio’s recovery factor. None of 54 VIX filter settings beat skipping the same share of days at random.

  1. US100 held long: the exit layers cut the maximum drawdown from −6.4 vol−$6,145−16.1% to −3.1 vol−$2,993−7.3%, against −16.8 for holding long at all hours. Evidence: Risk

Distances are multiples of the daily ATR at entry. “After price moves in the trade’s favor” is an adverse delayed entry: a long places a Buy Stop above, a short a Sell Stop below, and the order never fills if price does not get there, which skips signals that reverse right after the flip. Intraday strategies always close at the window end; for held strategies the window only gates the entry, each SuperTrend state enters once, and 60-70% of their trades are held overnight. The two gold intraday strategies are almost uncorrelated with the rest, so they still help; if real costs are higher than the stress case, they are the first to lose their edge.

How large can positions be on an FTMO 100K account?

Sizing has two levels. Per trade: every strategy uses the same base amount times its own weight, and lots = base × weight ÷ (stop multiple × daily ATR at entry × point value), floored to 0.01 lot, so a stop always loses a fixed amount, with no compounding. Across strategies: US100 and gold are two independent risk sources and each gets half of the total risk, split equally inside, so the two US100 strategies weigh 1.25 and the three gold strategies 0.83. US100.cash is USD 1 per point per lot; XAUUSD is 100 ounces per lot.

The base is set on 2022-2024 only, as the largest amount meeting three conditions: a daily loss (Prague days, floating losses included) within 80% of 4,500, a maximum drawdown within 80% of 10,000, and each of the five strategies’ largest sample loss happening on the same day still within 80% of 4,500. It comes to USD 700. A stop costs USD 875 for each US100 strategy (about 3 lots at the median) and USD 583 for each gold strategy (0.2-0.4 lot).

“Recent MAE” takes the 90th percentile of each strategy’s last 10 MAEs × 4 as the expected loss, 2-3 times the stop distance, so an actual stop loses only 30-40% of the budget; under the same limits the usable positions are much smaller, the profit is about half and the 2025-2026 recovery factor is lower. These strategies all have hard stops, so lots are better set by the stop distance. The weightings earn about the same, because the binding limit is the total risk of all stops on one day; grouping by instrument had the smallest drawdown and the highest recovery factor in 2025-2026, and needs no estimated parameter.

Why is the SuperTrend a risk switch?

In the root scan many cells beat holding through the same window; but when the same holding mask is shifted to other days of the calendar, the shifted versions often do just as well. On US100, tested on excess return, no family passed the 90% level on 1-hour, 4-hour or daily bars; the closest cell beat 85% of the shifts.

With the maximum drawdown as the statistic, the answer changes: time in the market stays the same and the shift only breaks the alignment with prices, so the question is whether the periods it picks are safer. For the US100 day session trend long, holding long through the window every session drew down 16.1, the same holding shifted to other days about 14, and the periods the SuperTrend picks only 5.6-7.1. Two of 8 families passed at 95%, against 0.4 expected by chance; on NAS100 from 2018-03 to 2022-02, never used for any choice, 5 of the plateau’s 6 cells passed too.

For the version whose window gates only the entry, the market is holding at all hours: the US100 trend long entering 08:30-13:30 has a 14-cell plateau, its best cell beating 100% of the shifts. Scanned from scratch on Pepperstone gold from 2018, the intraday version found a COMEX session long and a day session short, and the held version a long entering 09:30-16:00. Contrarian uses failed in every version; the gold held short had a low drawdown but lost money, so it was not used.

What did not work?

  • A VIX filter. 54 versions on the US100 strategy: the direction of the VIX daily and 4-hour SuperTrend, and the VIX level percentile. 28 lowered the drawdown, but only because they traded less; against skipping the same share of days at random (the VIX labels shifted around the calendar), none passed at 90%, the best reached 83%. The skipped trades still made money on average.
  • The SuperTrend as a source of excess return. On US100, no 1-hour, 4-hour or daily version, all hours or day session, passed the return test.
  • Contrarian uses. Shorting in uptrends or buying in downtrends lost to holding through the same window on both US100 and gold.
  • Different parameters of the same strategy together. Different exits or settings of the same entry signal have daily correlations of at least 0.64, median 0.90; they count as one strategy.

Can the portfolio be used?

Yes, after checking two things. The first is costs: the two US100 strategies and gold held long keep about 70% of their gain after stress costs and carry the portfolio; the two gold intraday strategies are thin and should be dropped if real spreads are wider than USD 0.60 an ounce. The second is sizing: on an FTMO 100K account each strategy uses a base of USD 700 with weights 1.25 for US100 and 0.83 for gold, and all stops on one day stay within 80% of the daily limit; the two held strategies bring more than half of the return and also carry weekend and overnight gaps. The portfolio’s value is being safer than holding the market in every year, not beating it on returns: in years when the market rallies strongly, it lags.

Questions this study answers

Does a 1-hour SuperTrend help as US100 entries and exits?

There is no evidence that it earns more: the best cell beat only 85% of the calendar shifts. There is evidence that it controls risk: for the same time in the market, the periods it picks have about half the drawdown of random periods, on US100 and on NAS100.

Can the VIX work as a filter?

No. None of 54 VIX filters beat skipping the same share of days at random, and the skipped trades still made money on average.

What is the final portfolio?

Five strategies: US100 intraday long, gold COMEX intraday long, gold intraday short, US100 held long and gold held long. Their daily correlations are between −0.16 and 0.08, and the combination made money every year since March 2022.

How large can positions be on an FTMO 100K account?

Each strategy uses the same base of USD 700 × its weight (US100 1.25, gold 0.83), with lots from the stop distance: a stop costs USD 875 on US100 and USD 583 on gold. From March 2022 to October 2026 it made USD 110,875 with reference costs, with a maximum drawdown of 5,333 and a worst day of 2,501.

Is there room for costs?

Yes. After reference costs and swaps it totals 125.7 with a maximum drawdown of 5.9; with costs 50% higher, 101.7 and 6.3. All five keep a positive net per trade in both periods; the two gold intraday strategies are the thinnest.

Questions raised along the way

Why both intraday and held versions?

The first design used the window for both entry and exit, so everything was intraday, and 60-70% of the roots’ trades were closed by the window end rather than the flip. With the window gating only the entry and the trade held to the flip, the median holding goes from 1.5-2.8 hours to 16-20 hours, trades fall by 40-60%, each trade earns more and costs weigh much less. The two versions’ daily correlations are near 0, so both stay in the portfolio.

Why are exit layers judged on drawdown rather than out-of-sample returns?

These strategies aim to lower risk, so a layer is kept whenever the backtest maximum drawdown falls below its parent’s, and results are compared with the market year by year. Under a rule of “choose on 2022-2024 and require a higher 2025-2026 recovery factor than the parent”, the US100 intraday take profit and breakeven would be dropped; either way the drawdown stays far below the market.

Why do the gold windows differ?

Each window formed its own plateau in the scan: the gold intraday long is steadiest in the COMEX day session 08:30-13:30, the short in New York 09:30-16:00, and the held long enters within 09:30-16:00. Holding gold long through the New York day session itself loses money, so the day-only long earns little, while the held version captures the overnight gains.

Maximum drawdown of the held periods' daily equity against every shift of the same holding mask around the calendar by 20 days or more; each cell becomes a z-score against its own shifts and is compared with the best shifted z of its whole use × window family; it passes at 90% or more, with a total at least the shift median and adjacent parameters passing too
Exit layers
Delayed entry → stop → take profit → breakeven → trailing stop; each layer is averaged over three adjacent SuperTrend settings and kept only if the maximum drawdown falls below its parent's; a stop is mandatory and sits at or beyond the MAE Q3; immediate entry is not accepted
