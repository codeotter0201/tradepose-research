Can the VIX filter or size US100 clock strategies? A test of the fear index out of sample

The VIX is the market’s price for the S&P 500’s expected volatility over the next 30 days, and it rises when traders pay up for protection. This study asks whether that information can decide when a US100 clock strategy should trade. The strategies are the 15 roots of the US100 entry and exit time study: fixed New York entry and exit times, immediate entry and no stop, so the only thing that changes is which days are traded. The filter reads the direction of a daily SuperTrend on the VIX from the previous completed New York day. A short trades only while the VIX trend is rising, a long only while it is falling. The question is whether this removes the right days, not just fewer days, and whether the effect holds across neighbouring settings and every year.

Conclusion

On the raw clock windows the VIX trend separates good and bad short days over 2022-2026 as a whole: the trades a rising-VIX filter skips barely paid, so the six shorts keep a higher profit factor and a shallower drawdown, across a plateau of SuperTrend settings. But the gain comes mostly from 2022-2024, and in 2025-2026 alone the filtered shorts have the lower recovery factor. Longs do best on rising-VIX days and should never wait for a falling VIX. Yet sizing positions by the VIX state, as a SuperTrend state or as the VIX-implied move relative to the ATR, did not beat the ATR sizing out of sample. The VIX carries information about US100’s days, but not enough, or not stably enough, to change these strategies.

  1. Tuesday short 08:00→11:00 with the VIX filter cuts its max drawdown from −3.7 vol−7.0% to −2.0 vol−3.7%. Evidence: Risk
  2. Over all twelve SuperTrend settings and the six shorts, the filtered strategies average a profit factor of 1.481.34, against 1.351.26 for the same shorts without the filter. Evidence: Parameter stability
  3. Monday long 18:00→12:00 falls from a total of +37.1 vol+54.5% to +12.6 vol+16.1% when it may only trade while the VIX trend is falling. Evidence: Returns

That is the conclusion. Each section below is the evidence for one claim; follow the sections or the evidence links into the strategy report for the data on every parameter set.

Which VIX:The VIX here is Pepperstone’s VIX CFD, which follows the front-month VIX futures, not the cash index. Its 15-minute bars are resampled to New York daily bars on the strategy’s own clock, and the filter only reads days that have closed.

How does the VIX filter work?

A SuperTrend draws a band around price at a multiple of its ATR and flips only when the close crosses the band. On the VIX daily bars this marks whether fear has been building or fading over the last few weeks, and its band widens when the VIX itself becomes jumpy, so a single spike does not flip it back and forth. The filter takes the direction of the previous completed New York day:

StrategyTrades only when the VIX SuperTrend is
ShortRising (fear building)
LongFalling (fear fading)

The article’s setting is ATR 15 trading days × 2.5. Every short and long root was also run with ATR 5, 15 and 20 days × 2.0, 2.5, 3.0 and 3.5, and with no filter, so the choice can be checked against its neighbours.

Is this better than skipping a random half of the days? For the pooled shorts, the filtered recovery factor was compared with the same keep/skip mask shifted around the calendar by every offset from 5% to 95% of the trades. At ATR 15 days × 2.5 it beats 97% of the shifts, and across the multipliers 2.5 and 3.0 with any of the three ATR lengths it beats 93% to 98%. The VIX and US100’s own daily SuperTrend agree about three times in four, yet the VIX filter still separates good and bad short days among the days US100’s own trend would allow, so it carries information beyond the index’s own trend.

Most of the gain comes from 2022-2024. In 2025-2026 alone the filtered shorts still earn more per trade, but their recovery factor is lower than without the filter (4.96 against 6.02 for the six raw shorts), because the thin trades it skips also smoothed the drawdowns. The ATR 15 days × 2.5 setting was chosen on the whole 2022-2026 history, so the full-period figures are in-sample.

Waiting for a falling VIX is the wrong way round: it removes the long windows’ best days, and in the chart’s table that combination has the lowest profit factor and the deepest max drawdown of the three. The rising-VIX days are the better long days, which fits buying a rebound while fear is still high: the rising-only combination has the highest profit factor and the smallest drawdown, and it is positive in every year. For longs, then, the VIX state looked like a case for sizing rather than filtering: a larger position on rising-VIX days and a smaller one on falling-VIX days would keep every profitable day. The next section tests that.

Can the VIX size positions instead of filtering?

A filter is a position of zero on the days it skips, so the wider question is whether the VIX state should scale positions up and down. Three tests kept every strategy’s ATR-based parameters and sizing and only multiplied the position by a VIX factor, with the factor estimated on earlier years and judged on later ones. Each factor followed the trades’ return per unit of variance in its state, averaged to 1 so that the average risk did not change:

TestVIX stateRecovery factor, ATR sizingWith the VIX factor
The 15 raw clock roots; factor from 2022-2024, judged on 2025-2026Daily SuperTrend (ATR 15 days × 2.5)11.7811.87
The 14 finished strategies of the clock strategy tree, USD sizing with a 4-point cost; factor from 2022-2024, judged on 2025-2026Daily SuperTrend (ATR 15 days × 2.5)17.9317.40
The same 14 strategies, refitted every year and judged on 2024-2026VIX-implied move ÷ ATR(72), terciles of the last 250 days16.0811.83

None of them beats the ATR sizing reliably. With the twelve SuperTrend settings, the second test improves on six and falls short on six. The third test used the only VIX feature, of four, that ranked the finished strategies’ trades the same way in 2022-2024 and in 2025-2026: trades taken when the VIX implied a larger move than the ATR earned more per unit of risk, which fits the ATR’s lag behind a change in volatility. But those trades were also more volatile, the pattern was not monotonic for longs, and a few years of data gave unstable factors; the rule beats only 10% of the same states shifted to other dates, and its result depends on the window: with a 120-day window instead of 250 it beats the ATR sizing, with 500 days it is about even. The VIX level and its direction against a 20-day average flipped between the two periods; the trades after a one-day VIX spike leaned the right way (longs better, shorts worse) in both periods, but on a few dozen trades.

Can the VIX replace the ATR as the volatility scale?

The VIX also looks like a better ruler for volatility than the ATR: it is forward-looking, while the ATR averages the past few months. Turned into an expected daily move (previous close × previous VIX ÷ 100 ÷ √252), it does anticipate the next session’s range better than the daily ATR(72) or a 143-day median true range:

Yet a better forecast did not make better strategies. The same 2,070 clock windows were scanned with the VIX scale and the roots chosen by the same rules: measured in percent of price, the 15 roots chosen with the VIX scale earned less than the 15 chosen with the ATR, with a smaller drawdown, because they missed two of the strongest Monday long windows. In 2022 the index fell while the VIX stayed low relative to the moves, so the VIX scale weighted that year’s trades too heavily. Delayed entries scaled by the VIX matched the ATR versions at the same distance in points and did no better. The VIX is useful here as a filter, not as the scale.

Read with caution:The clock roots and the filter settings were studied on the same 2022-2026 history, so the filter results are in-sample, and the out-of-sample checks above do not confirm them; the twelve settings and fifteen strategies are not independent tries. Costs are not included. Applied to the finished strategies of the clock and Market Profile weekday portfolio, which already have delayed entries, stops and Market Profile gates, the same filter did not help: the trades it would skip still earned after those exits. Trading the filter live also makes a US100 strategy depend on a second data feed. This is not trading advice.

What does this mean?

On the raw clock windows, a rising daily VIX trend has marked the days when shorting US100 pays, and the long windows have done best on rising-VIX days. But the short filter’s gain sits mostly in 2022-2024, and no way of sizing positions by the VIX, on raw windows or on finished strategies, beat the ATR sizing out of sample. For these strategies the ATR already captures most of what the VIX knows about volatility; the VIX stays out of the trading rules, and the one-day spike pattern is left to watch as more data comes in.

Questions this study answers

Can the VIX trend tell good short days from bad ones?

Yes. With the VIX filter the six short strategies keep 619619 of 1,4531,453 trades, raise their profit factor and cut their drawdown, and the combined recovery factor beats 97% of the same keep/skip mask shifted to other dates. The gain comes mostly from 2022-2024: in 2025-2026 alone the filtered shorts’ recovery factor is lower than without the filter.

Is the effect a plateau or a single lucky setting?

A plateau: with any ATR length of 5, 15 or 20 days and a multiplier of 2.5 or 3.0, the pooled shorts beat 93% to 98% of the shifted masks. Multipliers 2.0 and 3.5 are weaker.

Which VIX state suits the long strategies?

The rising one. Monday long 18:00→12:00 has a profit factor of 2.952.68 on rising-VIX days and 1.621.42 on falling-VIX days, and all nine longs point the same way. Because the falling-VIX days still earn, filtering them costs too much, and sizing longs by the state did not beat the ATR sizing out of sample either.

Does the VIX add anything beyond US100's own trend?

Yes. The two trends agree about three times in four, but among the short days that US100’s own daily SuperTrend allows, the VIX filter still keeps the better ones.

Questions raised along the way

Would a simpler VIX rule do the same job?

Not as well. A fast/slow moving-average crossover on the VIX (3-10 days against 20-60 days) points the same way but beats the shifted masks less often (median 74%) and never raises the shorts’ recovery factor; the VIX above its own long average is weaker still and flips about twenty times a year.

Should longs skip days when the VIX is unusually low, since prices then move less?

Prices do move less: in the lowest fifth of the VIX’s yearly range the clock trades moved about a third less. But after the delayed entries and stops of the finished strategies, those low-VIX long trades still earned after a 4-point cost, so a low-VIX veto did not pay either.

Why did the filter not help the finished strategies?

The skipped days earn almost nothing only for the raw clock windows. Once a strategy has a Market Profile gate, a delayed entry and a stop, the same days still earn on average, and the finished strategies were themselves chosen on a history that includes the falling-VIX days. A VIX layer has to be tested inside the tree, before the exits are chosen.

Can the VIX size positions instead of filtering?

Not reliably. Three tests kept the ATR parameters and sizing and added a VIX factor estimated on earlier years: on the raw clock roots it matched the ATR sizing, on the finished strategies it fell slightly short, and the VIX-implied move relative to the ATR, the only VIX feature stable across both periods, lost to time-shifted versions of itself in a yearly walk-forward test.
