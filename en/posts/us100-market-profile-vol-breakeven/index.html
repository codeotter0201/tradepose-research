Where should breakeven trigger? 9 US100 Market Profile setups

This is layer 5 of the Market Profile signals series: the breakeven stop (BE). The previous article is layer 4, Where to put the stop and the target?, which left 9 setups with a robust stop loss: Market Profile signals from the root study with a delayed entry, a SuperTrend × volatility veto and a stop loss (one also with a take profit). This layer adds only a breakeven stop and keeps everything else. G01–G16 are Market Profile event codes (for example, G09 = the open is above VAH); the glossary lists every condition, and the trigger and lock are multiples of the daily ATR at entry (volatility mode). These trades always close by 16:00 New York, so the last exit layer tests only a breakeven stop, not a trailing stop: once price has moved far enough in the trade’s favour, the stop moves to entry plus a small lock. The trigger means “a trade that gets this far no longer looks like a loser”, so it may only sit where few losing trades ever reach. Everything is measured in volatility mode.

Conclusion

Three of the nine setups keep a breakeven. G09 short at 06:30 gains the most and on the widest plateau; G13 N2 long at 10:30 improves on very few trades; G15 short at 06:30 passes the rule but the losses it saves are about as large as the profits it cuts. The other six keep their plain stop loss: for three of them no trigger beats the parent at all, and for the other three the good triggers are isolated points rather than a plateau.

  1. G09 short at 06:30 with trigger 0.26 and lock 0.03 raises the recovery factor from 7.093.92 to 9.817.18 and lowers the max drawdown from −3.5 vol−9.0% to −3.3 vol−6.6%, on the same trades. Evidence: Risk
  2. For G09 almost every trigger and lock beats the parent; the pick is chosen by score, and a neighbour (lock 0.02) has an even higher recovery factor of 10.947.58. Evidence: Parameter stability
  3. G15 short at 06:30 moves its recovery factor only from 2.131.23 to 2.341.30: a weak keep. Evidence: Returns

That is the conclusion. Each section below is the evidence for one claim; follow the sections or the evidence links into the strategy report for the data on every parameter set.

How does the breakeven stop work here?

Once the best price since entry passes entry plus the trigger (long; minus for a short), the stop moves to entry plus the lock. Both are multiples of the daily ATR at entry, like the stop loss. A trade that reaches the trigger and then reverses closes at a small profit instead of a loss; a winner that pulls back to the lock is cut there instead of running on.

The trigger starts at the parent’s losers’ G_MFE Q3: three quarters of the losing trades never got that far in their favour, so a trade that does has stopped looking like a loser. Lower triggers would fire on trades that are still ordinary losers and cut winners for nothing.

Why does the win rate jump?

Every kept breakeven raises the win rate sharply: G09 short from 39.5%39.5% to 54.1%54.1%. Much of that is mechanical: a breakeven exit closes at the lock, a small profit, so a trade that used to be a scratch or a loss now counts as a win. Read the win rate together with the profit factor and the recovery factor, which move far less.

Why do six setups keep no breakeven?

For G05 short at 08:30 and both G13 N1 longs (09:30 and 10:30), no trigger in the range beats the parent on both recovery factor and drawdown. For G04 N1 long at 08:30 and 09:30 and G08 long at 09:30, a few triggers do, but their neighbours do not, so the gain is an isolated point and the rule rejects it. These six keep the stop loss of the previous layer.

What does this mean?

A breakeven helps only where losing trades rarely reach the trigger but those that do often reverse. That holds clearly for G09 short at 06:30 and weakly for G15. Questions this study answers

Where should the breakeven stop trigger?

Where most losing trades never reach in their favour: the trigger is at least the parent’s losers’ G_MFE Q3 (75th percentile), so a trade that gets there no longer looks like a loser. Lower than that, the stop moves to breakeven on trades that are still ordinary losers and cuts winners for nothing.

Does a breakeven stop improve the 9 setups that already have a robust stop?

Three keep one, and only G09 short at 06:30 improves clearly: on the same trades its recovery factor rises from 7.093.92 to 9.817.18, and the neighbouring triggers and locks also beat the parent. G13 N2 long at 10:30 has very few trades, and G15 short at 06:30 only moves from 2.131.23 to 2.341.30.

Questions raised along the way

A breakeven stop lifts the win rate sharply. Can that judge the improvement?

Not on its own. A breakeven exit closes at the lock, a small profit, so trades that would have been scratches or small losses count as wins, and much of the jump is mechanical. The study judges by recovery factor and max drawdown instead, which move far less.

Some triggers beat the parent. Why do six setups still keep no breakeven?

For G05 short at 08:30 and both G13 N1 longs, no trigger beats the parent on both recovery factor and drawdown; for G04 N1 long at both times and G08 long at 09:30, only isolated triggers do, not their neighbours. The rule requires every trigger within ±0.04 at the same lock to beat the parent, so these six keep the stop loss of the previous layer.

Direction

Bars
US100 (FTMO) 15-minute execution, New York time; completed bars only
Parents
The 9 setups with a robust stop loss in the SL/TP layer: entry, SuperTrend x volatility veto and SL/TP unchanged, held to 16:00
Unit
Trigger, lock, SL and TP are multiples of the previous completed New York daily ATR (72 daily bars, about 60 trading days) at entry
Breakeven rule
Once the best price since entry passes entry ± trigger, the stop moves to entry ± lock
Trigger range
Every 0.02 from the losers' G_MFE Q3 to the winners' G_MFE Q2 of the parent, below the TP
Lock
0.01, 0.02, 0.03
Score
PF^0.4 × Sortino^0.3 × (win/loss ratio)^0.3 × expectancy retention^0.2 × MHL improvement^0.2 against the parent; each trigger averaged with its ±0.02 neighbours; 10th percentile of 2,000 day bootstraps
Robust
Expectancy positive in 2022–2024 and 2025–2026, and every trigger within ±0.04 at the same lock beats the parent on both recovery factor and max drawdown
Sample
New York entries from late January 2022 to September 23, 2026, Monday to Friday, on the days the veto keeps
Costs
Not included
