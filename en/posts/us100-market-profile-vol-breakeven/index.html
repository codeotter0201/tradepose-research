Where should breakeven trigger? 11 US100 Market Profile setups

This is layer 5 of the Market Profile signals series: the breakeven stop (BE). The previous article is layer 4, Where to put the stop and the target?, which left 11 setups with a robust stop loss: Market Profile signals from the root study with a delayed entry, the SuperTrend x volatility veto (daily SuperTrend for longs, 1h SuperTrend with low volatility for shorts) and a stop loss (three also with a take profit). This layer adds only a breakeven stop and keeps everything else. G01–G16 are Market Profile event codes (for example, G09 = the open is above VAH); the glossary lists every condition, and the trigger and lock are multiples of the daily ATR at entry (volatility mode). These trades always close by 16:00 New York, so the last exit layer tests only a breakeven stop, not a trailing stop: once price has moved far enough in the trade’s favour (the trigger), the stop moves to entry plus a small lock. The trigger means “a trade that gets this far no longer looks like a loser”, so it may only sit where few losing trades ever reach. Everything is measured in volatility mode.

How does the breakeven stop work here?

Once the best price since entry passes entry plus the trigger (long; minus for a short), the stop moves to entry plus the lock. Both are multiples of the daily ATR at entry, like the stop loss. A trade that reaches the trigger and then reverses closes at a small profit instead of a loss; a winner that pulls back to the lock is cut there instead of running on.

The trigger starts at the parent’s losers’ G_MFE Q3: three quarters of the losing trades never got that far in their favour, so a trade that does has stopped looking like a loser. Lower triggers would fire on trades that are still ordinary losers and cut winners for nothing.

Why does the win rate jump?

Every kept breakeven raises the win rate sharply: G09 short at 06:30 from 39.9%39.9% to 54.5%54.5%. Much of that is mechanical: a breakeven exit closes at the lock, a small profit, so a trade that used to be a scratch or a loss now counts as a win. Read the win rate together with the profit factor, which moves far less (G09 short at 06:30 from 1.331.26 to 1.541.45), and with the recovery factor and max drawdown.

Why do three setups keep no breakeven?

For G04 N1 long at 08:30 (one bar closes below VAL, the next closes back above), G05 short at 08:30 (a break below the profile low that closes back above) and G14 long at 09:30 (a break above the profile high that closes back below), no trigger in the range beats the parent on both recovery factor and drawdown. For G05 short and G14 long at 09:30, no trigger even raises the recovery factor; for G04 N1 long, some triggers raise the recovery factor and others lower the drawdown, but never both at once. These three keep the stop loss of the previous layer.

What does this mean?

A breakeven helps where losing trades rarely reach the trigger but those that do often reverse into a full stop. Here it lowers the max drawdown of all eight setups that keep one; for G11 long at 09:30 and G13 N1 long at 10:30 it also costs a little total return, so there it buys risk, not profit. The final parameters of every setup, with and without the filter and split by weekday, are in the series conclusion, From signal to final strategy.

Questions this study answers

Where should the breakeven stop trigger?

Where most losing trades never reach in their favour: the trigger is at least the parent’s losers’ G_MFE Q3 (75th percentile), so a trade that gets there no longer looks like a loser. Lower than that, the stop moves to breakeven on trades that are still ordinary losers and cuts winners for nothing. Four of the eight kept triggers sit right at that lower bound.

Does a breakeven stop improve the 11 setups that already have a robust stop?

Eight keep one, and in all eight the max drawdown falls on the same trades. G09 short at 06:30 improves most: its recovery factor rises from 7.514.38 to 10.097.10, on a wide plateau of robust triggers and locks. G11 long at 09:30 only moves from 2.522.10 to 2.852.75 and loses some total return.

Questions raised along the way

A breakeven stop lifts the win rate sharply. Can that judge the improvement?

Not on its own. A breakeven exit closes at the lock, a small profit, so trades that would have been scratches or small losses count as wins, and much of the jump is mechanical. The study judges by recovery factor and max drawdown instead, and the profit factor moves far less than the win rate.

Why do three setups still keep no breakeven?

For G04 N1 long at 08:30, G05 short at 08:30 and G14 long at 09:30, no trigger beats the parent on both recovery factor and drawdown; for G05 short and G14 long at 09:30, none even raises the recovery factor. The rule requires every trigger within ±0.04 at the same lock to beat the parent on both, so these three keep the stop loss of the previous layer.

Unit
Trigger, lock, SL and TP are multiples of the previous completed New York daily ATR (72 daily bars, about 60 trading days) at entry
Breakeven rule
Once the best price since entry passes entry ± trigger, the stop moves to entry ± lock
Trigger range
Every 0.02 from the losers' G_MFE Q3 to the winners' G_MFE Q2 of the parent, below the TP
Lock
0.01, 0.02, 0.03 (below the trigger)
Score
PF^0.4 × Sortino^0.3 × (win/loss ratio)^0.3 × expectancy retention^0.2 × MHL improvement^0.2 against the parent; each trigger averaged with its ±0.02 neighbours; 10th percentile of 2,000 day bootstraps
Robust
Expectancy positive in 2022–2024 and 2025–2026, and every trigger within ±0.04 at the same lock beats the parent on both recovery factor and max drawdown
Sample
New York entries from late January 2022 to September 23, 2026, Monday to Friday, on the days the filter keeps
