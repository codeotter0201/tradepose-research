Can a delayed entry cut MAE? 36 US100 Market Profile setups

This is layer 1 of the Market Profile signals series: delayed entry. The previous article is the root study, Do Market Profile signals work on both sides of value?, which picked 36 Market Profile setups (signal + direction + entry time), each entering as soon as its event fires and holding to 16:00; this layer changes only the entry, and keeps the signals, exits and sample. The question is whether waiting helps. One option is a favorable delayed entry: a Buy/Sell Limit, where a long places a limit order below the signal price and fills on a pullback. The other is an adverse delayed entry: a Buy/Sell Stop, where a long places a stop order above the signal price and fills on a breakout. Distances are multiples of the daily ATR (volatility mode). G01–G16 are Market Profile event codes (for example, G10 = a whole bar closes above VAH); the glossary lists every code’s condition. The target is the maximum adverse excursion (MAE): under fixed-risk sizing a smaller MAE means a tighter stop and a larger position, so the score weighs the MAE cut double against the cost in favourable excursion.

Conclusion

For 15 of the 36 setups a delayed entry scores above zero, and 8 of those keep their benefit when chosen on 2022–2024 and tested on 2025–2026. Every one of the 15 raises the profit factor and lowers the max drawdown, but total return barely changes, because a delayed order misses part of the trades. Almost all gains come from the favorable delayed entry; the adverse delayed entry helps only a few longs. The other 21 setups keep the near-immediate favorable delayed entry at 0.02.

How is a delayed entry scored?

Each candidate is compared with the immediate entry on the same signal days. The score is twice the cut in mean MAE plus the change in mean G_MFE, both as log changes, so a 10% MAE cut is worth a 20% G_MFE gain. Under fixed-risk sizing the stop, and so the position size, scales with MAE, and G_MFE / MAE is the reward potential per unit of risk. Each distance is averaged with its ±0.02 neighbours, because a limit or stop order rarely fills exactly where planned, and the score is the 10th percentile over 2,000 resamples of the trading days. An entry must fill at least 30% of the signals and make money in both 2022–2024 and 2025–2026.

This article shows each setup’s immediate entry, its pick, and only the delayed entries that score above zero with enough fills; the full grid is in the research record.

Why is the favorable delayed entry almost always the choice?

Eleven of the 15 picks are favorable delayed entries, and every short pick is one. An adverse delayed entry waits for a breakout, so it enters at a worse price and, in these setups, usually with a larger MAE; it pays off only for a few longs late in the session (G08 at 09:30 and 10:30, G09 and G11 at 10:30 or 09:30), where waiting for the breakout filters out failed starts.

Why is the MAE drop or the expectancy alone not enough?

The same grid gives different impressions depending on what is measured, so this layer picks per setup by the MAE-weighted score:

  • The overall medians at a fixed distance hide the difference. Across the 36 setups, a favorable delayed entry at 0.36 fills about 40% of signals and cuts the median MAE by 10.9%, while G_MFE falls too. Judged by the MAE drop alone, the favorable delayed entry does little.
  • An adverse delayed entry improves expectancy, not MAE. At 0.20, 14 of the 36 setups raise expectancy in both periods, because losing trades are filtered out; yet the median MAE rises by 1.5%, and by 5.1% for shorts.
  • The score puts both on one scale. The MAE improvement counts twice, less the G_MFE loss. A favorable delayed entry lowers MAE and G_MFE by about a tenth each, still a net gain at double weight; an adverse delayed entry mostly does not lower MAE, and only some longs score above zero.

So this article reports the per-setup picks under this layer’s goal, the MAE-weighted score, rather than the overall medians at a fixed distance.

Where does the grid end?

Two first picks sat on the 0.36 edge, so their grids were extended to 0.50. G10 short at 06:30 stays at 0.36: farther limits score lower and eventually lose money. G09 long at 10:30 moves to 0.34 once its neighbours on both sides are known.

Read with caution:The picks were chosen from 18–25 distances per kind after looking at the data. G04 N2 short at 09:30 and 10:30 have fewer than 100 signals. A delayed order that does not fill is a missed trade, not a loss, so profit factor and win rate improve partly because the hard days are skipped. Costs are not included. This is not trading advice.

What does this mean?

A favorable delayed entry (waiting for a pullback) is a cheap way to cut the adverse excursion of these setups, at the cost of fewer trades rather than lower profit. The picks go on to layer 2: the SuperTrend × volatility filter.

Questions this study answers

Can a delayed entry cut MAE without giving up return?

For 15 of the 36 setups a delayed entry scores above zero, and 8 of them still gain when chosen on 2022–2024 and tested on 2025–2026. All 15 raise the profit factor and lower the max drawdown while total return stays roughly flat: G10 short at 06:30 with a favorable delayed entry at 0.36 cuts the max drawdown from −20.6 vol−35.8% to −7.1 vol−12.8%. The cost is fewer filled signals, not less profit.

Favorable or adverse delayed entry: which suits these setups?

The favorable delayed entry (Buy/Sell Limit, waiting for a pullback) is 11 of the 15 picks and every short pick. The adverse delayed entry (Buy/Sell Stop, waiting for a breakout) helps only a few late-session longs: G09 long at 10:30 with an adverse delayed entry at 0.34 raises the profit factor from 1.2241.248 to 1.7381.855, while its total return falls.

Questions raised along the way

The overall medians say a favorable delayed entry barely lowers MAE, yet it wins most picks. Why?

The two measure different things. In the overall medians at a fixed distance, a favorable delayed entry lowers MAE and G_MFE by about a tenth each; the score counts the MAE improvement twice, so that is still a net gain. An adverse delayed entry raises expectancy while MAE rises, and only some longs score above zero. This layer picks per setup by the MAE-weighted score.

Limit and stop orders rarely fill exactly where planned. How does the score handle that?

Each distance is averaged with its ±0.02 neighbours, and the score is the 10th percentile of 2,000 bootstraps over trading days. A pick must also fill at least 30% of the signals and have positive expectancy in both 2022–2024 and 2025–2026.

Two setups first picked 0.36, the edge of the grid. What happened?

Their grids were extended to 0.50 and the pick was rerun. G10 short at 06:30 stays at 0.36, since farther limits score lower and eventually lose money; G09 long at 10:30 moves to 0.34 once its neighbours on both sides are known.
