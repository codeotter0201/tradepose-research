Market benchmarks for 22 instruments: what simply holding earns by day, week and month

A backtest result means little on its own: a long strategy on a rising index earns money partly because the index rose. This article builds the market benchmark of 22 instruments, the plain strategy of staying in the market long or short over every day, week and month, so that any strategy can be compared with what simply holding the same instrument, side and calendar would have earned.

Conclusion

Simply holding long made money on most of the 2222 instruments from mid-2022 to September 2026, and holding short lost money on almost all of them, so beating the short benchmark does not by itself mean a short strategy makes money. The longer the holding period, the shallower the drawdown counted on closed trades looks, but most of that is drawdown hidden inside open monthly trades.

  1. Holding long every day ended positive on 1921 of 2222 instruments; holding short every day ended positive on 31. Evidence: Returns
  2. Across the 22 long markets, the mean max drawdown on closed trades is −12.0 vol−22.7% for monthly holds against −17.8 vol−31.8% for daily holds, but counting open losses it is −15.9 vol−28.9% against −18.5 vol−33.2%. Evidence: Risk
  3. Even holding long month by month, only 68 of the 22 instruments ended every calendar year from 2022 to 2026 positive. Evidence: Consistency over time

That is the conclusion. Each section below is the evidence for one claim; follow the sections or the evidence links into the strategy report for the data on every parameter set.

How is the market strategy built?

The market strategy holds one side of one instrument over every period and re-enters at the start of the next one. Each period is one trade: entry at the open of the period’s second 15-minute bar, exit at the open of the next period’s first bar. Every overnight gap, daily break and weekend therefore sits inside a trade, and the only time not held is the first 15 minutes of each period, which the backtest engine needs to close one trade and open the next.

Holding periodOne trade perTypical length
Daytrading session, open to the next openabout 24 hours; Friday to Sunday about 72
Weekthe sessions dated in one calendar weekabout 168 hours
Monththe sessions dated in one calendar monthabout 730 hours

The periods come from each instrument’s own bars, not from one clock. Most CFD feeds pause for an hour every day, and that pause starts the next session. Where a feed fills the pause in some years, or leaves long gaps inside the day, the session open is declared instead (the list is in Methods and data below). A session is dated by the day it mostly covers, so the US index session that opens at 18:00 New York on Sunday is Monday’s. Holidays inside a week do not split it; when a market is closed for several days, the trade simply runs through the closure.

Before the table was used, every instrument, period and side was checked: one trade per period, at least 99% of the time held, no periods merged or split by the feed, and the short side the exact mirror of the long side before costs.

Which market benchmark should a strategy be compared with?

Compare a strategy with the market strategy of the same instrument and broker feed, the same side, the same calendar and the same unit, on the holding period that matches how long the strategy stays in: an intraday strategy with the day market, a strategy holding up to a week with the week market, and longer holds with the month market. A long strategy is compared with the long market and a short strategy with the short market.

A strategy is usually in the market only part of the time, so the comparison is made in three ways rather than on total return alone:

ComparisonStrategyMarketWhat it answers
Return per hour in the markettotal ÷ hours heldthe same, for the matching marketdoes the strategy earn more for each hour it holds risk than simply holding?
Max drawdownits max drawdown, and its share of time in the marketthe market’s max drawdowna drawdown that falls only in line with the time in the market is arithmetic, not risk control
Total, overall and per yearits totalthe market’s total × the strategy’s share of time in the marketwhat the same exposure to the market would have paid; the years the strategy fell behind are listed

For example, a long US100 strategy that holds 14% of the time should earn more per hour than the day market below, its max drawdown should be well under 14% of the market’s, and its yearly totals should beat 14% of the market’s yearly totals.

How do day, week and month holds differ?

The three holding periods cover almost the same hours, so their totals are close; the differences come from the first 15 minutes of each period, which is not held. A day market leaves out about 250 such bars a year, a month market twelve, and on instruments whose sessions reopen after a break those bars carry the reopening move.

Drawdown is where they really differ. The max drawdown counted on closed trades only sees a monthly trade when it closes, so a month market hides every fall that recovers before month end: its mean is −12.0 vol−22.7% against −17.8 vol−31.8% for the day market. Counting the losses of open trades (unrealized drawdown), the month market’s mean rises to −15.9 vol−28.9%, close to the day market’s −18.5 vol−33.2%. When a strategy’s drawdown is compared with a market of a different holding period, use the unrealized drawdown on both sides.

Which unit should the benchmark be read in?

Read the benchmark in the strategy’s own unit. In volatility mode each trade’s P&L is divided by the instrument’s daily volatility at exit, which gives every trade the same weight in risk terms and lets instruments with different prices and volatility sit in one table. The benchmark comes in two volatility scales, the simple mean of the last 72 daily True Ranges and their median over 143; pick the one the strategy uses. Percent returns, switched at the top of the page, do not depend on the scale.

Read with care:

All results are before costs: no spread, commission, overnight swap, or the roll adjustment brokers apply to CFDs that track futures (VIX, crude oil, copper, the dollar index and several indexes). With costs the short market is no longer the mirror of the long market. Each broker’s feed has its own prices and hours, so compare a strategy with the market on the same feed. The sample is one stretch, June 2022 to September 2026, which includes one bear market in indexes and strong rises in gold. Returns are simple sums of per-trade results without position sizing or compounding. This is a research reference, not investment advice.

Questions this study answers

What does simply holding each instrument earn by day, week and month, long and short?

Holding long made money on most instruments in every holding period, with the indexes and gold far ahead of currencies, crude oil and the dollar index, while holding short lost money on almost all of them. The tables above give each instrument’s total, drawdown and yearly results in both units.

How should a strategy be compared with its market?

Against the market of the same instrument, feed, side, calendar and unit, on the holding period that matches the strategy’s, through three comparisons: return per hour in the market, max drawdown against the share of time in the market, and the total, overall and per year, against the market’s total at the same exposure.

Questions raised along the way

Why does the EUR/USD and USD/JPY day turn at 16:00 New York and not at the usual 17:00?

At the 17:00 rollover the quoted price dips for a few minutes as spreads widen, then recovers within the hour. A day turning at 17:00 would leave that recovery on the one bar the market strategy does not hold, every day, and make the long side look worse than the currency moved. Turning the day at 16:00 keeps the dip and the recovery inside the same trade; the weekend, from Friday 16:00 to the Sunday reopen, becomes a session of its own.

Why are HK50 and CN50 cut by their local clock?

In 2022 these feeds quoted around the clock from the Monday reopen, so there was no daily break to find. Their day turns at 06:00 Hong Kong or Shanghai time, an hour later feeds leave empty, and is dated by the local date, which also keeps the cut fixed when New York changes to and from daylight saving time.

Can the unit change the conclusion?

Yes. Volatility mode weighs a trade in a calm stretch more than the same price move in a turbulent one, so a market that rose mostly in volatile periods can be negative in volatility units and positive in percent; switch the unit at the top of the page to see the VIX month market do exactly that. This is why the benchmark must be read in the strategy’s own unit.

Why are some indexes listed twice?

FTMO and Pepperstone quote the same US indexes and gold with their own prices, hours and gaps. A strategy built on one broker’s bars is compared with the market on that broker’s bars.
