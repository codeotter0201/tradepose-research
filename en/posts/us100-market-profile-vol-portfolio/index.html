Portfolio and position size: 8 US100 Market Profile strategies on a 100k FTMO account, with spread and slippage

This is the portfolio article of the Market Profile signals series. The previous article is the series conclusion, From signal to final strategy, which walks through every layer from the root signals to the breakeven stop and leaves eleven strategies; this article does not change the strategies, only how they are traded together. G01–G16 are Market Profile event codes (for example, G09 = the open is above VAH); the glossary lists every condition. Trading them together raises three questions: which ones are really the same bet, how much do spread and slippage take, and how large can each position be on a 100,000 FTMO account without risking the 10,000 total-loss limit in a bad year. Every strategy risks a fixed amount per trade; nothing compounds.

Conclusion

Three strategies are dropped for moving with a stronger one: G13 N1 long at 10:30, G08 short at 08:30 and G11 long at 09:30. The remaining eight stay profitable under both cost assumptions, though G09 long at 10:30 loses most of its edge to costs. With 1,000 USD of risk per trade, the conservative-cost chance of a 10,000 drawdown within a year stays under 5%, and the worst day stays far inside the 4,000 daily limit. Every year is positive in both volatility mode and USD; 2022 is the weakest year in USD, because the sizing formula needs ten trades of history before it sizes a strategy, and the early 2022 trades that were left unsized were profitable on balance.

  1. The eight strategies together earn +104.5 vol+158.2%+$83,476 without costs and +64.8 vol+94.6%+$47,801 under the conservative costs, over 1,9551,9551,875 trades. Evidence: Returns
  2. G09 long at 10:30 (the open is above VAH) pays the most for costs relative to its edge: its profit factor falls from 1.3371.3571.291 to 1.1091.1341.069. Evidence: Risk
  3. After the three drops, the eight are different bets: the most correlated pair among them has a daily correlation of only 0.290.290.30. Evidence: Diversification

That is the conclusion. Each section below is the evidence for one claim; follow the sections or the evidence links into the strategy report for the data on every parameter set.

Which strategies go together?

How large can each trade be?

Each trade risks a fixed USD amount: the lots are set so that the expected loss, four times the 90th percentile of the strategy’s recent MAE in ATR units, costs that amount. The risk per trade is the largest value on a 500–1,300 grid for which the chance of a 10,000 drawdown within a year, from resampled 5-day blocks of the daily results, stays at or below 5% under the conservative costs. That gives 1,000 USD per trade: the conservative-cost probability is about 4.7%, and 1,100 would already exceed 5% (about 7.4%). No trade was skipped by the daily-loss check, and the worst day lost about 1,390 USD under the conservative costs, about a third of the 4,000 limit.

Switch the page to USD to see the results at 1,000 per trade.

Why is 2022 the weakest year in USD?

The sizing formula needs ten completed trades of each strategy before it can estimate the expected loss, so the first ten trades of every strategy have no position: 80 trades, 77 of them in 2022. At the base cost those unsized trades earned about 7.0 of the 10.2 volatility units the eight made in 2022, mostly from the first ten trades of G05 short at 08:30; the sized rest of 2022 earned about 3.2. So in USD 2022 stays positive but is much smaller than the other years. This is the formula’s warm-up, not a weakness of the strategies; a live account starts with that history already available.

What does this mean?

Eight strategies from the Market Profile signals series fit together on a 100,000 account at 1,000 USD of risk per trade, and stay under the total-loss limit even with conservative costs, though with a thin margin over the 5% probability line. The next step before trading is out-of-sample time and real account costs.

Questions this study answers

Which of the eleven final strategies are really the same bet?

Three pairs, each trading the same area of the previous day’s profile: G13 N1 long at 10:30 with G11 long at 10:30 (daily correlation 0.63), G08 short at 08:30 with G09 short at 06:30 (0.58), and G11 long at 09:30 with G09 long at 10:30 (0.52). The rule drops the one with the lower recovery factor in each pair, leaving eight strategies whose most correlated pair has a daily correlation of 0.290.290.30.

Do they still make money after spread and slippage?

All eight stay profitable under both cost assumptions: together +104.5 vol+158.2%+$83,476 without costs and +64.8 vol+94.6%+$47,801 under the conservative costs. Strategies with a thin edge per trade are hit hardest; G09 long at 10:30 falls from a profit factor of 1.3371.3571.291 to 1.1091.1341.069.

How large can each position be on a 100,000 FTMO account?

1,000 USD of risk per trade: on a 500–1,300 grid, that is the largest amount for which the conservative-cost chance of a 10,000 drawdown within a year stays at or below 5% (about 4.7%). The daily-loss check skipped no trade, and the worst day, about 1,390 USD, stays far inside the 4,000 daily limit.

Questions raised along the way

How is the drawdown probability estimated without relying on a single historical path?

By resampling the daily results in 5-day blocks 5,000 times and counting how often the drawdown reaches the total-loss limit within a year, under the conservative costs. It only resamples the same history and does not cover regimes that history did not contain.

Why is 2022 so much smaller in USD than in volatility mode?

The sizing formula needs ten completed trades of each strategy to estimate the expected loss, so the first ten trades (77 of the 80 in 2022) have no position; at the base cost they earned about 7.0 of the 10.2 volatility units of 2022, so the sized trades kept only a small part of that year’s profit. This is the formula’s warm-up; a live account starts with that history already available.

Method and data

Research setup, data and calculations; the identifiers needed to reproduce the study are under data sources.

Research setup
Article type
Single-strategy study
Instrument
US100.cash
Direction
Both directions
Strategies
The 11 final strategies of the series conclusion (last stage with the SuperTrend × volatility veto)
Correlation
Daily results in R (P&L ÷ stop distance); while any pair correlates above 0.5, drop the one with the lower recovery factor
Costs
Index points per lot, assumptions: base = spread 1.5, stop slippage 2.0, market-exit slippage 0.5; conservative = 3.0 / 4.0 / 1.0; limit orders (TP, favorable entries) fill at their price
Sizing
Fixed risk per trade, no compounding: lots = risk ÷ expected loss in points, expected loss = 4 × the 90th percentile of the last 10 trades' MAE in ATR × the entry ATR; the first 10 trades of each strategy have no position
Account
FTMO 100,000; total loss limit 10,000; daily loss limit 4,000 (checked); US100.cash 1 USD per point per lot, lots in 0.01
Risk choice
The largest risk per trade on a 500–1,300 grid whose one-year probability of a 10,000 drawdown stays at or below 5% under the conservative cost (5-day block bootstrap of daily results, 5,000 draws)
Unit
Volatility mode by default; percentage and USD as switches
Sample
New York Base entries from late January 2022 to September 23, 2026, Monday to Friday
Parameters
Signal: G04 N1 / G05 / G08 / G09 / G11 / G13 N1 / G14 / G16; Direction: Long / Short; Entry time: 06:30 / 08:30 / 09:30 / 10:30; Portfolio: Kept / Dropped; Cost: No cost / Base / Conservative
Return calculation
Volatility-normalized: Sum of per-trade P&L ÷ volatility at exit (exit_volatility); Percentage: Sum of per-trade returns (simple); USD: Each trade sized by risk_amount $1,000 ÷ the rolling expected loss, USD P&L summed (trades without enough history to estimate the expected loss have no position and are left out)
