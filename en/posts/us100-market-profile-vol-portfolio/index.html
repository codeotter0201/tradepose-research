Portfolio and position size: 8 US100 Market Profile strategies on a 100k FTMO account, with spread and slippage

This is the portfolio article of the Market Profile signals series. The previous article is the series conclusion, From signal to final strategy, which walks through every layer from the root signals to the breakeven stop and leaves nine strategies; this article does not change the strategies, only how they are traded together. G01–G16 are Market Profile event codes (for example, G09 = the open is above VAH); the glossary lists every condition. Trading them together raises three questions: which ones are really the same bet, how much do spread and slippage take, and how large can each position be on a 100,000 FTMO account without risking the 10,000 total-loss limit in a bad year. Every strategy risks a fixed amount per trade; nothing compounds.

Conclusion

One strategy is dropped for moving with another: G04 N1 long at 09:30, which correlates with G04 N1 long at 08:30. The remaining eight stay profitable under both cost assumptions, though G08 long at 09:30, the most active, loses much of its edge to costs. With 900 USD of risk per trade, the conservative-cost chance of a 10,000 drawdown within a year stays under 5%, and the worst day stays far inside the 4,000 daily limit. In USD the first year is slightly negative, because the sizing formula needs ten trades of history before it sizes a strategy, and the early 2022 trades that were left unsized happened to be the profitable ones.

  1. The eight strategies together earn +132.2 vol+203.6%+$82,378 without costs and +75.0 vol+109.2%+$44,248 under the conservative costs, over 2,3962,3962,316 trades. Evidence: Returns
  2. G08 long at 09:30 has the most trades and pays the most in costs: its profit factor falls from 1.2571.2731.244 to 1.0561.0761.045. Evidence: Risk
  3. The two G04 N1 longs are close to the same bet: daily correlation 0.510.460.49. Evidence: Diversification

Which strategies go together?

Two strategies that win and lose on the same days add risk without adding diversification. The daily results in R are compared pair by pair; while any pair correlates above 0.5, the one with the lower recovery factor is dropped. Only one pair crosses the line: G04 N1 long at 09:30 and at 08:30, the same event an hour apart. The 08:30 version has the higher recovery factor (4.352.424.10 against 2.393.522.56), so it stays.

How large can each trade be?

Each trade risks a fixed USD amount: the lots are set so that the expected loss, four times the 90th percentile of the strategy’s recent MAE in ATR units, costs that amount. The risk per trade is the largest value on a 500–1,300 grid for which the chance of a 10,000 drawdown within a year, from resampled 5-day blocks of the daily results, stays at or below 5% under the conservative costs. That gives 900 USD per trade: the conservative-cost probability is about 3.7%, and 1,000 would already exceed 5%. No trade was skipped by the daily-loss check, and the worst day lost about a quarter of the 4,000 limit.

Switch the page to USD to see the results at 900 per trade.

Why is the first year negative in USD?

The sizing formula needs ten completed trades of each strategy before it can estimate the expected loss, so the first ten trades of every strategy, almost all in early 2022, have no position. Those unsized trades were profitable in volatility mode, while the sized rest of 2022 was slightly negative. So in USD 2022 ends slightly down while every year is positive in volatility mode. This is the formula’s warm-up, not a weakness of the strategies; a live account starts with that history already available.

What does this mean?

Eight strategies from the Market Profile signals series fit together on a 100,000 account at 900 USD of risk per trade, with a modest margin under the total-loss limit even with conservative costs. The next step before trading is out-of-sample time and real account costs.

Questions this study answers

Which of the nine final strategies are really the same bet?

Only one pair: G04 N1 long at 08:30 and at 09:30, the same event an hour apart, with a daily correlation of 0.510.460.49. The rule drops the 09:30 version, which has the lower recovery factor, leaving eight strategies.

Do they still make money after spread and slippage?

All eight stay profitable under both cost assumptions: together +132.2 vol+203.6%+$82,378 without costs and +75.0 vol+109.2%+$44,248 under the conservative costs. Strategies with many trades and stop exits are hit hardest; G08 long at 09:30 falls from a profit factor of 1.2571.2731.244 to 1.0561.0761.045.

How large can each position be on a 100,000 FTMO account?

900 USD of risk per trade: on a 500–1,300 grid, that is the largest amount for which the conservative-cost chance of a 10,000 drawdown within a year stays at or below 5%. The daily-loss check skipped no trade, and the worst day stays far inside the 4,000 daily limit.

Questions raised along the way

How is the drawdown probability estimated without relying on a single historical path?

By resampling the daily results in 5-day blocks 5,000 times and counting how often the drawdown reaches the total-loss limit within a year, under the conservative costs. It only resamples the same history and does not cover regimes that history did not contain.

Why is the first year negative in USD while every year is positive in volatility mode?

The sizing formula needs ten completed trades of each strategy to estimate the expected loss, so the first ten trades (almost all in early 2022) have no position; those trades happened to be profitable, while the sized rest of 2022 was slightly negative. This is the formula’s warm-up; a live account starts with that history already available.

Method and data

Research setup, data and calculations; the identifiers needed to reproduce the study are under data sources.

Research setup
Article type
Single-strategy study
Instrument
US100.cash
Direction
Both directions
Strategies
The 9 final strategies of the series conclusion (last stage with the SuperTrend × volatility veto)
Correlation
Daily results in R (P&L ÷ stop distance); while any pair correlates above 0.5, drop the one with the lower recovery factor
Costs
Index points per lot, assumptions: base = spread 1.5, stop slippage 2.0, market-exit slippage 0.5; conservative = 3.0 / 4.0 / 1.0; limit orders (TP, favorable entries) fill at their price
Sizing
Fixed risk per trade, no compounding: lots = risk ÷ expected loss in points, expected loss = 4 × the 90th percentile of the last 10 trades' MAE in ATR × the entry ATR; the first 10 trades of each strategy have no position
Account
FTMO 100,000; total loss limit 10,000; daily loss limit 4,000 (checked); US100.cash 1 USD per point per lot, lots in 0.01
Risk choice
The largest risk per trade on a 500–1,300 grid whose one-year probability of a 10,000 drawdown stays at or below 5% under the conservative cost (5-day block bootstrap of daily results, 5,000 draws)
Unit
Volatility mode by default; percentage and USD as switches
Sample
New York Base entries from late January 2022 to September 23, 2026, Monday to Friday
Parameters
Signal: G04 N1 / G05 / G08 / G09 / G13 N1 / G13 N2 / G15; Direction: Long / Short; Entry time: 06:30 / 08:30 / 09:30 / 10:30; Portfolio: Kept / Dropped; Cost: No cost / Base / Conservative
Return calculation
Volatility-normalized: Sum of per-trade P&L ÷ volatility at exit (exit_volatility); Percentage: Sum of per-trade returns (simple); USD: Each trade sized by risk_amount $900 ÷ the rolling expected loss, USD P&L summed (trades without enough history to estimate the expected loss have no position and are left out)
