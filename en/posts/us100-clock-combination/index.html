US100 clock strategy tree: 10 strategies grown from 2,069 entry/exit windows, stops placed by the MAE distribution, breakevens judged by the MAE they avoid

One clock window can grow many strategies: different delayed entries, different stops, with or without breakeven, with or without a filter. This study treats each window as the root of a tree, keeps several branches that pass at every layer, and only once every strategy has grown screens them together into a combination. Two judgements matter most along the way: where should a stop go so it does not cut trades that would come back, and a breakeven gives up some profit, but does it actually spare us the adverse excursion? All figures are in volatility mode: each trade’s P&L divided by the daily ATR(72) at exit (the Average True Range over 72 New York daily bars, about 60 trading days), summed, and every stop, target and delayed-entry distance is a multiple of it; see the research glossary.

How does the strategy tree grow?

The study has five layers, each changing one thing:

LayerChangesA child must pass
RootEntry and exit timeBeat its own control and drift; form a plateau on the parameter grid
Delayed entryFavorable or adverse delayed-entry distanceFavorable: conservative estimate of the MAE-weighted score (10th bootstrap percentile) > 0, positive expectancy in both halves; adverse: the filter rules (beat the circular-shift control, higher recovery factor in both halves, unfilled days really lose); drawdown below the parent’s either way
SL/TPStop (always) and targetConservative estimate of the exit score > 0; every ±0.04 neighbour lowers the drawdown; the stopped trades mostly lose
BreakevenTrigger and lockThe breakeven exits avoided MAE; profit per unit of MAE better in both halves
FiltersTrend and Market ProfileBeat its own control; the filtered trades mostly lose

The delayed-entry layer keeps up to 5 children of each kind per root, the later layers up to 2 per node; neither correlation nor room per trade plays any part in the choice, both wait for the portfolio stage once every strategy is complete. Filters come last because they decide which days not to trade; whether they block bad days can only be judged once the entry and exit rules are fixed.

Roots: how many real plateaus among 2,069 windows?

Each cell is tested on its own: it must beat its randomized control and the average of windows with the same holding time (ruling out simply riding a rising index). Passing cells adjacent on the 30-minute entry × exit grid are joined into one plateau; a lone cell is an island and is dropped. 21 of 28 plateaus hold; the Monday long plateau is the largest, stretching from Sunday night to Monday noon.

Delayed entry: favorable, adverse and the fallback

Delayed entry aims to lower the MAE each trade carries. A favorable delayed entry places a limit order on the favorable side of the signal price, a Buy Limit below it for longs and a Sell Limit above it for shorts, and fills on a pullback. It misses exactly the winners that run away at once, so its unfilled trades are not checked. An adverse delayed entry places a stop order on the adverse side, a Buy Stop above the signal price for longs and a Sell Stop below it for shorts, and fills once price has moved first. It does a different job: it pays a worse price for a confirmation and skips the days that start in the wrong direction. Judged by the MAE score it is bound to fail, because from the worse entry point the G_MFE is mechanically short by the entry distance and the MAE does not fall. So adverse delayed entries use the filter rules instead: the fill mask must beat its own circular-shift control, the recovery factor must beat the parent’s in both halves, the drawdown must be lower and the unfilled days must consistently lose. 7 of the 21 roots pass, all at small distances (0.02-0.12): further out, the cost of the worse price eats the confirmation’s benefit. Favorable delayed entries keep up to 5 per root, but that adds only 6 branches on three roots, as the other roots pass fewer than 3 distances anyway. When no favorable distance passes, a favorable delayed entry of 0.02 is used.

Stops: why always, and where?

A stop is necessary: without one, the worst trade can lose more than a full ATR. But it must not sit inside the adverse move trades go through anyway. On the Monday 20:30→12:30 long, one winner in ten has an MAE above 0.49 × ATR and the largest is close to 3; a stop at 0.52 cuts several trades that would have ended in profit. So stop candidates run from the winners’ MAE Q3 up to all trades’ MAE Q95, and when nothing passes in full, the fallback stop sits beyond the winners’ MAE Q90 at the best profit per unit of MAE. This one ends at 0.72 with a max drawdown of −1.5 vol−2.3%−$1,846, against −1.2 vol−2.5%−$1,068 without a stop after delayed entry: the stop caps every loss at the cost of some drawdown and profit.

How the stop multiple is chosen. Every distance is a multiple of the daily ATR at entry, so the stop widens on volatile days; the evidence is each trade’s MAE after delayed entry, before any stop. Take the Monday 18:00→14:00 long (favorable delayed entry 0.28):

  1. Candidate range. The lower end is the smaller of the winners’ MAE Q3 and all trades’ MAE Q2 (at least three winners in four never touch the stop), and never below 3 times the median 15-minute bar range; the upper end is all trades’ MAE Q95. Steps are 0.02, widening to 0.04 above all trades’ MAE Q3; take-profits are tested at the winners’ G_MFE Q1-Q3 and without one. Here the range is 0.28 to 1.04, 26 stops.
  2. Three checks for every candidate. Robust: the neighbouring stops within ±0.04 also bring the max drawdown below the no-stop version, so the choice sits on a plateau, not a single point. Halves: split the sample in two, and the expectancy is positive in both. The stopped trades really are bad: the trades that hit the stop would have lost anyway in most years and in both halves.
  3. Ranking. The survivors are ranked on robustness, max drawdown, worst year, recovery factor, R per trade and how bad the stopped trades are; the average rank decides, keeping at most two that differ by at least 0.04.

This one lands at 0.68: the winners’ MAE Q90 is 0.49 and no winner ever moved more than 0.68 against the position; 13.6% of all trades go beyond 0.68 and are cut. Without a stop the worst trade lost 1.49 × ATR; with it no trade loses more than 0.68. Each strategy’s stop multiple comes from its own MAE distribution, so the ten differ.

Breakeven: what does giving up profit buy?

A breakeven gives up profit: once triggered, a return to the entry price closes the trade, and many such trades would have turned into winners. Its purpose is not more profit but avoiding the losing path. So a breakeven is judged on the trades it closes: without it, how much further would their MAE have grown? Most breakeven settings do avoid MAE, but they give up even more profit; few improve profit per unit of MAE in both halves and only 5 nodes pass; the breakeven of the Thursday 19:30→00:30 long (trigger 0.14, lock 0.02 × ATR) survives the portfolio’s correlation pruning, but that strategy earns only 11 points per trade and the cost test keeps it out; the others do not outrank the no-breakeven branch of their root. Trades that would have turned profitable after a breakeven exit belong to a different holding logic; this study does not count them as breakeven failures and judges only by profit per unit of MAE.

Filters: applied once the strategy is complete

Filters are tested after SL/TP, again against their own control and requiring the blocked trades to mostly lose. Two of the final 10 keep one. The adverse-entry branch of the Thursday 07:00→11:00 short takes an RTH profile filter (RTH is the regular US trading session, 09:30-16:00 New York; no short when the previous RTH session’s Market Profile value area already sits below): the adverse delayed entry first asks price to move 0.10 × ATR in the short direction, the filter then removes the days with no room left to fall, lifting the profit factor from 1.5221.4961.402 to 2.0011.9071.839; the same filter on the immediate or favorable delayed entry fails, because the days it blocks do not consistently lose. The Thursday 06:30→16:00 short keeps the 1-hour SuperTrend × volatility filter (in low volatility, no short while the SuperTrend is bullish), which lifts its profit factor from 1.4311.3491.343 to 1.7471.6131.645.

Cost test: why the five strategies earning 10-12 points per trade are left out

Together the ten total 142.7 R at 6 points of cost, with a max drawdown of 8.75, a recovery factor of 16.3 and every year still positive.

How are positions sized on an FTMO 100K account?

The ten are registered for live trading with these settings: lots follow the stop distance, so every stop-out costs a fixed amount; risk parity runs over three trading-day clusters, Monday, Tuesday and Thursday, with multipliers of 1.18, 1.12 and 0.70 on a base of USD 743, so a stop-out costs 877, 832 or 520; the multipliers are checked every quarter and change only when they drift out of range. Under the 4,500 daily and 10,000 total loss limits, the total loss binds first: the max drawdown on daily closing equity is USD 9,986, just inside 10,000. Why MAE is not used for sizing, how six sizing formulas and six allocations compare, and how the cluster multipliers are computed and maintained are covered in Position sizing and risk allocation for an FTMO account.

Caveats
  • Returns are simple sums of per-trade results. Volatility mode (each trade’s P&L divided by the daily ATR at exit) and percentage mode ignore position size; USD mode sizes every trade with the registered settings of the sizing section and takes 4 points of cost off each; no mode compounds. In USD mode the clock and delayed-entry layers, which have no stop yet, take 1 × ATR as the stop distance, for comparison only.
  • Roots, branches and filters were all chosen on the same sample (2022 to September 2026), so every result is in-sample; each layer’s control estimates selection bias but does not replace new data.
  • In the buy-and-hold comparison the combination sums 10 strategies carrying one unit of volatility risk each, while buy-and-hold carries one unit; their exposures differ.
  • The Monday 18:00→19:00 long has only 82 trades and a mean just above the threshold; it is borderline.
  • The adverse delayed-entry branch of the Thursday 07:00→11:00 short was picked from 36 entry distances, 52 stop candidates and 12 profile gates, so its selection bias is larger than the others’.
  • The cost test uses a fixed round-trip cost in points; it ignores the half spread a favorable delayed entry’s limit order may save and the wider spreads around news.
  • The four Monday longs, the two Tuesday longs and the three Thursday shorts each overlap in holding time; the sizing settings’ worst-case day already assumes a day’s positions stop out together.
  • The sizing base of USD 743 and the cluster multipliers are upper bounds inferred from the in-sample max drawdown; a longer losing run out of sample is possible, so they are not a guarantee.
  • The report shows the filter as part of the strategy definition; the result equals blocking those sessions on the finished strategy.
  • Apart from the cost test section, returns include no spreads, commissions or slippage.
  • Not investment advice.

Questions this study answers

Which robust strategies can one clock window grow, and how do they combine?

The 21 window plateaus grow 83 finished strategies; the portfolio selection and the cost test keep 10: four Monday longs, two Tuesday longs, a Tuesday short and three Thursday shorts. With one unit of volatility risk each, the 10 total 173.9 R with a max drawdown of 7.10, are positive in every year from 2022 to 2026 and beat that year’s buy-and-hold in every year.

Where should a stop go so it does not cut trades that would come back?

Outside the winners’ MAE cluster: at least the winners’ MAE Q3 (75th percentile) or all trades’ MAE Q2, and when no candidate passes, a fallback stop beyond the winners’ MAE Q90. The Monday 20:30→12:30 long with its stop at 0.72 × ATR goes from a profit factor of 3.4823.1323.205 to 2.8202.5882.609, in exchange for a cap on every trade’s loss.

A breakeven gives up profit; does it spare us the losing path?

Most breakeven settings do avoid MAE, but they give up even more profit; only 5 nodes improve profit per unit of MAE in both halves. The one that survives the correlation pruning, on the Thursday 19:30→00:30 long, earns only 11 points per trade and the cost test keeps it out, so none of the final 10 uses a breakeven.

Questions raised along the way

An MAE score is bound to reject adverse delayed entries; how are they judged?

From the worse entry price the G_MFE is mechanically short by the entry distance and the MAE does not fall. The study judges them by the filter rules instead: the fill days must beat their own circular-shift control, the recovery factor must beat the parent’s in both halves, the drawdown must be lower and the unfilled days must really lose. 7 of the 21 roots pass, all at small distances of 0.02-0.12 × ATR.

Should strategies earning only 10-12 points per trade join the portfolio?

No. All five trade in the Asian and early European sessions, where spreads are wider; at 4 points of round-trip cost their net profit falls to 6-8 points per trade and three have losing years. What they add is mostly diversification: at 6 points the recovery factor is 16.9 with them and 16.3 without. The study therefore sets the room-per-trade gate at 0.04 × daily ATR (about 12 points).

Can several branches of one window be traded together?

Not when they differ only in stop or filter: their daily returns correlate above 0.85, which amounts to stacking the same trade. Branches with different entries can: the adverse and favorable delayed-entry branches of the Thursday 07:00→11:00 short correlate at 0.4, and the favorable delayed entries at 0.28 and 0.08 of the Monday 18:00→14:00 long at 0.48; the combination keeps both pairs.

Why are filters, room per trade and correlation judged last?

Filters decide which days not to trade, and entry and exit rules change every day’s result, so only a finished strategy shows whether a filter blocks bad days. Room per trade and correlation are demands on a finished strategy; applied earlier they would cut whole roots before their branches grow. So all 21 roots go through the five layers first, and the 83 finished strategies are screened together.

Session
Daily break 17:00-18:00 New York; a session runs from the 18:00 open to 17:00 the next day, and the Sunday 18:00 open belongs to Monday
Roots
The 2,069 clocks of the US100 entry and exit time study, every day and each weekday, long and short; every cell must beat its own control (every day: best-of zero-edge resampling; weekday: random days) and same-holding drift; passing cells adjacent on the 30-minute grid form one plateau, lone cells are dropped, and each plateau's best-ranked cell is its root
Order
Clock -> delayed entry -> SL/TP -> breakeven -> filters; filters are applied after the strategy is complete
Branches
Up to 5 delayed-entry children of each kind per root, then up to 2 children per node per layer, ranked by mean rank of robustness, max drawdown, worst year, recovery factor, R per trade, profit per unit of MAE and the filtered trades' results; correlation is not used during selection, only afterwards for the portfolio
Child gates
Beat its own control or score P10 > 0; positive expectancy in both halves; max drawdown below the parent's; at least 70 trades; filters, adverse delayed entries and stops must remove trades that lose in most years and in both halves (favorable delayed entries are not checked: their unfilled trades are winners that ran away); room per trade and correlation are not judged inside the tree
Delayed entry
Favorable and adverse 0.02-0.36 × daily ATR, judged separately: favorable delayed entries by the MAE-weighted score; adverse delayed entries are a confirmation filter paid for with a worse price, judged by the filter rules (the fill mask beats its own circular-shift control, recovery factor above the parent in both halves, lower drawdown, unfilled days really lose); up to 5 of each kind; favorable 0.02 when no favorable entry passes
SL/TP
A stop always exists; at least 3 × the 15-minute bar range and at least the winners' MAE Q3 or all trades' MAE Q2, up to all trades' MAE Q95; when nothing passes, the fallback stop sits beyond the winners' MAE Q90 with the best profit per unit of MAE
Breakeven
Trigger at least 3 bars of range and the losers' G_MFE Q3; the breakeven exits must have avoided MAE (most years, both halves) and profit per unit of MAE must beat the parent in both halves
Portfolio selection
Every finished strategy of the 21 roots enters the pool (83); each root keeps its top 3 with daily-return correlation below 0.6 (mean rank of recovery factor, max drawdown, worst year, R per trade, profit factor and win rate); across the pool, in rank order, anything correlated 0.6 or more with a kept strategy is dropped; only then must each trade earn at least 0.04 × daily ATR (about 12 points) on average; the cost test sets this gate
Sizing
FTMO 100K account, total loss at most USD 10,000 and at most 4,500 in a day; fixed risk, no compounding; lots follow the stop distance; risk parity over Monday, Tuesday and Thursday clusters, a stop-out costs USD 877, 832 or 520; method in Position sizing and risk allocation for an FTMO account
Mode
Volatility mode by default: each trade's P&L divided by the daily ATR at exit, summed; switch to percentage at the top of the page
Sample
Sessions from 2022 to 23 September 2026
Costs
Not included in the main results; the cost test section takes 0-6 points per round trip off every trade

Strategy: 10 values, full list in the strategy report; Filter: None / RTH profile not below / 1h ST × volatility; Stage: 0 Clock / 1 Delayed entry / 2 Stop loss
