US100 clock strategy tree: 14 strategies grown from 2,070 entry/exit windows, stops placed by the MAE distribution, breakevens judged by the MAE they avoid

One clock window can grow many strategies: different delayed entries, different stops, with or without breakeven, with or without a filter. This study treats each window as the root of a tree, keeps several branches that pass at every layer, and only once every strategy has grown screens them together into a combination. Two judgements matter most along the way: where should a stop go so it does not cut trades that would come back, and a breakeven gives up some profit, but does it actually spare us the adverse excursion? All figures are in volatility mode: each trade’s P&L divided by the daily ATR(72) at exit (the Average True Range over 72 New York daily bars, about 60 trading days), summed, and every stop, target and delayed-entry distance is a multiple of it; see the research glossary.

How does the strategy tree grow?

The study has five layers, each changing one thing:

RootEntry and exit timeBeat its own control and drift; form a plateau on the parameter grid

FiltersTrend and Market ProfileBeat its own control; the filtered trades mostly lose

The delayed-entry layer keeps up to 5 children of each kind per root, the later layers up to 2 per node; neither correlation nor room per trade plays any part in the choice, both wait for the portfolio stage once every strategy is complete. Filters come last because they decide which days not to trade; whether they block bad days can only be judged once the entry and exit rules are fixed.

Delayed entry: favorable, adverse and the fallback

Delayed entry aims to lower the MAE each trade carries. A favorable delayed entry places a limit order on the favorable side of the signal price, a Buy Limit below it for longs and a Sell Limit above it for shorts, and fills on a pullback. It misses exactly the winners that run away at once, so its unfilled trades are not checked. An adverse delayed entry places a stop order on the adverse side, a Buy Stop above the signal price for longs and a Sell Stop below it for shorts, and fills once price has moved first. It does a different job: it pays a worse price for a confirmation and skips the days that start in the wrong direction. Judged by the MAE score it is bound to fail, because from the worse entry point the G_MFE is mechanically short by the entry distance and the MAE does not fall. So adverse delayed entries use the filter rules instead: the fill mask must beat its own circular-shift control, the recovery factor must beat the parent’s in both halves, the drawdown must be lower and the unfilled days must consistently lose. 4 of the 15 roots pass: the Monday 22:30→10:00 long at 0.10, the Tuesday 08:00→11:00 short at 0.02, the Thursday 06:30→16:30 short at 0.30 and the Thursday 08:00→10:30 short at 0.16. Favorable delayed entries keep up to 5 per root; five roots keep more than one, 23 branches in all, four of them on the Monday 18:30→14:00 long. When no favorable distance passes, as on 7 of the 15 roots, a favorable delayed entry of 0.02 is used.

Stops: why always, and where?

A stop is necessary: without one, the worst trade of the Monday 18:00→12:00 long loses 1.52 × ATR. But it must not sit inside the adverse move trades go through anyway. On the Wednesday 18:00→22:30 long, one winner in ten has an MAE above 0.13 × ATR and the largest reaches 0.57; any stop in that range cuts trades that would have ended in profit. So stop candidates run from the winners’ MAE Q3 up to all trades’ MAE Q95, and when nothing passes in full, the fallback stop sits beyond the winners’ MAE Q90 at the best profit per unit of MAE. None of this window’s 7 stops (0.14 to 0.36) passes in full; the fallback lands at 0.16 and still cuts 8 of the 91 winners, with a max drawdown of −0.6 vol−1.4%−$7,120, against −0.5 vol−1.4%−$1,144 without a stop after delayed entry: the stop caps every loss at the cost of some drawdown and profit.

How the stop multiple is chosen. Every distance is a multiple of the daily ATR at entry, so the stop widens on volatile days; the evidence is each trade’s MAE after delayed entry, before any stop. Take the Monday 18:00→12:00 long (favorable delayed entry 0.06):

  1. Candidate range. The lower end is the smaller of the winners’ MAE Q3 and all trades’ MAE Q2 (at least three winners in four never touch the stop), and never below 3 times the median 15-minute bar range; the upper end is all trades’ MAE Q95. Steps are 0.02, widening to 0.04 above all trades’ MAE Q3; take-profits are tested at the winners’ G_MFE Q1-Q3 and without one. Here the range is 0.28 to 0.94, 23 stops.
  2. Three checks for every candidate. Robust: the neighbouring stops within ±0.04 also bring the max drawdown below the no-stop version, so the choice sits on a plateau, not a single point. Halves: split the sample in two, and the expectancy is positive in both. The stopped trades really are bad: the trades that hit the stop would have lost anyway in most years and in both halves.
  3. Ranking. The survivors are ranked on robustness, max drawdown, worst year, recovery factor, R per trade and how bad the stopped trades are; the average rank decides, keeping at most two that differ by at least 0.04.

This one keeps two: 0.78 without a target, and 0.32 with a target at 0.34. At 0.78 the winners’ MAE Q90 is 0.49 and only one winner ever moved further than 0.78 against the position; 8.1% of all trades go beyond 0.78 and are cut. Without a stop the worst trade lost 1.52 × ATR; with it no trade loses more than 0.78. Each strategy’s stop multiple comes from its own MAE distribution, so across the fourteen they range from 0.16 to 0.90.

Breakeven: what does giving up profit buy?

A breakeven gives up profit: once triggered, a return to the entry price closes the trade, and many such trades would have turned into winners. Its purpose is not more profit but avoiding the losing path. So a breakeven is judged on the trades it closes: without it, how much further would their MAE have grown? Across the 1,074 breakeven settings tested on 38 nodes, 799 do avoid MAE in most years and in both halves, but they give up even more profit; only 18 settings, on 5 nodes, improve profit per unit of MAE in both halves, and none of those passes the robustness check (a positive conservative score, with every neighbouring trigger also lowering the drawdown). No breakeven passes, so every finished strategy exits by its stop, its target or the clock. Trades that would have turned profitable after a breakeven exit belong to a different holding logic; this study does not count them as breakeven failures and judges only by profit per unit of MAE.

Filters: applied once the strategy is complete

Filters are tested after SL/TP, again against their own control and requiring the blocked trades to mostly lose. Three of the final 14 keep one. The favorable-entry branch of the Thursday 08:00→10:30 short takes an RTH profile filter (RTH is the regular US trading session, 09:30-16:00 New York; it shorts only when price at entry sits above the previous RTH session’s Market Profile value area, with room left to fall back into it): it keeps 99 of 229 trades and lifts the profit factor from 1.3441.2801.227 to 2.4812.3392.241; the same filter on the adverse delayed-entry branch of that window fails, because the days it would block made money in total. The Wednesday 18:00→22:30 long takes the opposite RTH profile filter (no long when price at entry sits above the previous RTH value area), which lifts its profit factor from 1.8061.7651.456 to 2.5932.4232.116. The Thursday 06:30→16:30 short keeps the 1-hour SuperTrend × volatility filter (in low volatility, no short while the SuperTrend is bullish), which lifts its profit factor from 1.4611.3991.372 to 1.7961.6841.692. No filter passes on any adverse delayed-entry branch.

How are positions sized on an FTMO 100K account?

Positions for the fourteen are set as follows: lots follow the stop distance, so every stop-out costs a fixed amount; risk parity runs over four trading-day clusters, Monday (seven strategies), Tuesday (two), Wednesday (one) and Thursday (four), with multipliers of 0.60, 1.03, 1.76 and 0.60 from the latest estimate (from 21 July 2026) on a base of USD 785, so a stop-out costs 471, 809, 1,382 or 471. The Wednesday cluster holds a single strategy, so risk parity gives it the largest multiplier. The multipliers are checked every quarter and change only when they drift out of range. Under the 4,500 daily and 10,000 total loss limits, the total loss binds first: the max drawdown is USD 9,987, just inside 10,000, while a day on which every open position stops out costs at most about 3,500. Caveats
  • Returns are simple sums of per-trade results. Volatility mode (each trade’s P&L divided by the daily ATR at exit) and percentage mode ignore position size; USD mode sizes every trade with the settings of the sizing section and takes 4 points of cost off each; no mode compounds. In USD mode the clock and delayed-entry layers, which have no stop yet, take 1 × ATR as the stop distance, for comparison only.
  • Roots, branches and filters were all chosen on the same sample (2022 to September 2026), so every result is in-sample; each layer’s control estimates selection bias but does not replace new data.
  • In the buy-and-hold comparison the combination sums 14 strategies carrying one unit of volatility risk each, while buy-and-hold carries one unit; their exposures differ.
  • The Monday 18:00→19:00 long has only 91 trades and a mean just above the threshold, and the adverse delayed-entry branch of the Thursday 08:00→10:30 short sits right at it (0.040 × ATR); both are borderline.
  • The favorable-entry branch of the Thursday 08:00→10:30 short was picked from 36 entry distances, 18 stop candidates and 16 filters, and its filter keeps only 99 of 229 trades, so its selection bias is larger than the others’.
  • The cost test uses a fixed round-trip cost in points; it ignores the half spread a favorable delayed entry’s limit order may save and the wider spreads around news.
  • The seven Monday longs and the four Thursday shorts each overlap in holding time; the sizing settings’ worst-case day already assumes a day’s positions stop out together.
  • The sizing base of USD 785 and the cluster multipliers are upper bounds inferred from the in-sample max drawdown; the Wednesday multiplier rests on a single strategy of 111 trades; a longer losing run out of sample is possible, so they are not a guarantee.
  • The report shows the filter as part of the strategy definition; the result equals blocking those sessions on the finished strategy.
  • Apart from the cost test section, returns include no spreads, commissions or slippage.

Questions this study answers

Which robust strategies can one clock window grow, and how do they combine?

The 15 window plateaus grow 53 finished strategies; the portfolio selection and the cost test keep 14: seven Monday longs, a Tuesday long, a Tuesday short, a Wednesday long and four Thursday shorts. With one unit of volatility risk each, the 14 total 214.0 R with a max drawdown of 7.91, are positive in every year from 2022 to 2026 and beat that year’s buy-and-hold in every year.

Where should a stop go so it does not cut trades that would come back?

Outside the winners’ MAE cluster: at least the winners’ MAE Q3 (75th percentile) or all trades’ MAE Q2, and when no candidate passes, a fallback stop beyond the winners’ MAE Q90. The Wednesday 18:00→22:30 long with its fallback stop at 0.16 × ATR goes from a profit factor of 2.5952.4522.076 to 1.8061.7651.456, in exchange for a cap on every trade’s loss.

A breakeven gives up profit; does it spare us the losing path?

Most breakeven settings do avoid MAE (799 of 1,074), but they give up even more profit; only 18 settings on 5 nodes improve profit per unit of MAE in both halves, and none of them passes the robustness check. No breakeven passes, so none of the final 14 uses one.

Questions raised along the way

An MAE score is bound to reject adverse delayed entries; how are they judged?

From the worse entry price the G_MFE is mechanically short by the entry distance and the MAE does not fall. The study judges them by the filter rules instead: the fill days must beat their own circular-shift control, the recovery factor must beat the parent’s in both halves, the drawdown must be lower and the unfilled days must really lose. 4 of the 15 roots pass, at distances of 0.02-0.30 × ATR, and three of them reach the final portfolio.

Should strategies earning only 8-10 points per trade join the portfolio?

No. All three trade in the Asian and early European sessions, where spreads are wider; at 4 points of round-trip cost their net profit falls to 4-6 points per trade and each has losing years. They do not help the portfolio either: its recovery factor is lower with them at every cost, 13.6 against 15.7 at 6 points. The study therefore sets the room-per-trade gate at 0.04 × daily ATR (about 12 points).

Can several branches of one window be traded together?

Usually not when they differ only in stop or filter: their daily returns mostly correlate above 0.85, which amounts to stacking the same trade. A much tighter stop with a target is the exception: the Monday 18:00→12:00 long with a stop at 0.78 and with a stop at 0.32 plus a target at 0.34 correlate at only 0.37. Branches with different entries can coexist too: the favorable and adverse delayed-entry branches of the Thursday 08:00→10:30 short correlate at 0.48, and the favorable delayed entries at 0.24 and 0.20 of the Monday 18:30→14:00 long at 0.58; the combination keeps all three pairs.

Why are filters, room per trade and correlation judged last?

Filters decide which days not to trade, and entry and exit rules change every day’s result, so only a finished strategy shows whether a filter blocks bad days. Room per trade and correlation are demands on a finished strategy; applied earlier they would cut whole roots before their branches grow. So all 15 roots go through the five layers first, and the 53 finished strategies are screened together.

Order
Clock -> delayed entry -> SL/TP -> breakeven -> filters; filters are applied after the strategy is complete
Branches
Up to 5 delayed-entry children of each kind per root, then up to 2 children per node per layer, ranked by mean rank of robustness, max drawdown, worst year, recovery factor, R per trade, profit per unit of MAE and the filtered trades' results; correlation is not used during selection, only afterwards for the portfolio
Child gates
Beat its own control or score P10 > 0; positive expectancy in both halves; max drawdown below the parent's; at least 70 trades; filters, adverse delayed entries and stops must remove trades that lose in most years and in both halves (favorable delayed entries are not checked: their unfilled trades are winners that ran away); room per trade and correlation are not judged inside the tree
Delayed entry
Favorable and adverse 0.02-0.36 × daily ATR, judged separately: favorable delayed entries by the MAE-weighted score; adverse delayed entries are a confirmation filter paid for with a worse price, judged by the filter rules (the fill mask beats its own circular-shift control, recovery factor above the parent in both halves, lower drawdown, unfilled days really lose); up to 5 of each kind; favorable 0.02 when no favorable entry passes
SL/TP
A stop always exists; at least 3 × the 15-minute bar range and at least the winners' MAE Q3 or all trades' MAE Q2, up to all trades' MAE Q95; when nothing passes, the fallback stop sits beyond the winners' MAE Q90 with the best profit per unit of MAE
Breakeven
Trigger at least 3 bars of range and the losers' G_MFE Q3; the breakeven exits must have avoided MAE (most years, both halves) and profit per unit of MAE must beat the parent in both halves
Portfolio selection
Every finished strategy of the 15 roots enters the pool (53); each root keeps its top 3 with daily-return correlation below 0.6 (mean rank of recovery factor, max drawdown, worst year, R per trade, profit factor and win rate); across the pool, in rank order, anything correlated 0.6 or more with a kept strategy is dropped; only then must each trade earn at least 0.04 × daily ATR (about 12 points) on average; the cost test sets this gate
Sizing
FTMO 100K account, total loss at most USD 10,000 and at most 4,500 in a day; fixed risk, no compounding; lots follow the stop distance; risk parity over Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday clusters, a stop-out costs USD 471, 809, 1,382 or 471; method in Position sizing and risk allocation for an FTMO account

Volatility mode by default: each trade's P&L divided by the daily ATR at exit, summed; switch to percentage at the top of the page
