Conclusion

The question: while the SuperTrend points one way, does holding with it (or against it) inside a session window control drawdown better than simply holding? Following the strategy-selection methodology from 768 candidates gave a 10-strategy portfolio with +USD 70,078 after cost and a max drawdown of 4,723 in the selection period. Read once after the freeze, the validation period lost USD 2,564, and 7 of the 10 strategies had deeper drawdowns than their random-timing versions. The roots (the state signal alone, no exit rules) earned about zero per trade after cost in selection; the profit came from the entry and exit tree, whose contribution fell from +0.040 to +0.006 volatility units per trade in validation. Along the way an engine problem was found and fixed: 4h bars used to depend on whether a Run started in daylight-saving time.

That is the conclusion. Each section below is the evidence for one claim; follow the sections or the evidence links into the strategy report for the data on every parameter set.

Question and hypothesis

Written before any data was read:

  1. Phenomenon: while the SuperTrend is in one direction, a position in that direction has a shallower drawdown than one of the same exposure at random times; no clear edge per trade. Holding against the state is the control hypothesis.
  2. Anchor: the bar (1h or 4h) on which the SuperTrend turns to that direction, inside the session window.
  3. Holding: intraday = held inside the window until the flip or the window close; held = entered inside the window, held until the flip.
  4. Expected size: near zero per trade; the claim is the drawdown.
  5. Abandon if: the drawdown is not shallower than random timing at the same exposure and the t value is within the noise ceiling.

Candidates

192 per instrument: 1h and 4h SuperTrend × intraday and held × four uses (long in bullish, short in bearish, long in bearish, short in bullish) × 4 ATR lengths (1, 3, 9, 27 trading days) × 3 multipliers (1.5, 2.5, 3.5).

The 12 cells sharing instrument, timeframe, holding and use form a family. Noise ceilings, design checks and thresholds are per family; each family sends one representative cell forward.

The “rolled versions” are the random-timing benchmark: the SuperTrend state is shifted by K whole sessions (same time of day), so the number, length and overnight holds of the trades stay the same and only the dates change. Forty values of K per candidate.

Problems met and how they were handled

ProblemHandling
The state roll counted K × a fixed number of bars, which drifts off the same time of day when sessions have fewer barsRoll by whole sessions, each bar reading the same time of day K sessions earlier; checked session by session on the engine, every non-holiday session aligned
The positive control’s cheat (the day’s direction) has nothing to do with state timing, so it survived the roll and the timing rows could never flag itRoll the cheat together with the state; the path row then flagged 77–100% of the cheating candidates
Forty rolled versions for every candidate is expensiveCompute the rows that need one Run per candidate first; roll only the candidates whose disposition still depends on it
4h bars used to depend on whether a Run’s first bar was in daylight-saving time, so two Runs with different starts got different 4h bars on the same dayReported and fixed in the engine: 2h–12h bars are now cut on the New York wall clock all year (see How to build 4-hour bars); every stage that used 4h bars was rerun, and 1h and daily results were unchanged trade for trade
A saved Experiment of the same name was matched only by member count, so changed parameters could reuse the old definitionExperiment names carry a hash of the members, recipe source, parameters and period

Results by stage

Calibration: all 32 families of the main instruments were unreadable. The smallest effect worth trading is 0.043–0.048 units per trade against a per-trade standard deviation of 0.45–2.08; reading it needs hundreds to tens of thousands of trades per cell, against 100 to 800 available. 29 families had their best t inside the noise band. Exploration continued under the disposition rule, which selects on drawdown rather than mean return.

Veto list: 368 admissible candidates → 83 with the market and single-instrument rows passing → 50 with the path row as well, in 13 families. Trend-following families passed most, in connected plateaus; the passing cells of the counter-trend families mostly hit “condition adds nothing”.

Tree and leaves: 13 roots grew 228 leaves; after the year, regime and against-the-root checks 10 roots went on, and the top 3 leaves of each (28) were rolled: 27 had drawdowns shallower than 80–100% of their rolled versions. Every leaf had at least one layer on its path with a bootstrap stability below 70%.

Combination: leaves of one root are highly correlated, so each root keeps one; all 10 went into the portfolio.

  1. The state signal has no mean edge: the roots earned about zero per trade after cost in selection, as the calibration said a mean effect could not be read.
  2. The exit parameters were chosen on the same data: outside r13, the exit rules’ contribution fell from +0.040 to +0.006 on average. Every leaf had a layer with stability below 70%: its best parameter changes with the sample.
  3. The rolled comparison favours the real timing: the exits were tuned on the real trades and the rolled versions merely reuse them, so part of the selection-period lead came from the tuning.
  4. The 2026 market: several roots lost about 0.05 units per trade in validation, and win rates fell by about 5 to 10 points.

Decision

  1. The 10-strategy portfolio is not deployed.
  2. r14 is kept on watch: 45 trades, +USD 1,359, drawdown 2.70 units in validation, shallower than its market version (3.72) and 90% of its rolled versions. It was picked after reading the validation period, so it is a new candidate, frozen 2026-10-24 without a clean validation; its clean record starts 2026-10-26, and after 3 to 6 months the post-freeze record decides keep, reduce or stop.
  3. What r14’s evidence says: its root (short in bearish, no exits) lost in both periods, −0.035 and −0.033; the profit is from the exit rules (+0.125, +0.093). It shows that this exit set still worked in validation, not that the bearish SuperTrend state itself has an edge.

from tradepose_client import ExperimentMember, Policy, authoring as tp
from playbook.strategies.research.supertrend_state_session_trade import (
    SupertrendStateSessionTradeParams as P, supertrend_state_session_trade as recipe)

r14 = ExperimentMember(
    id="r14_us100_4h_bear_short_hold", source=recipe,
    params=P(primary=P.Primary(instrument="FTMO:US100.cash"),
             trend=P.Trend(freq=tp.Freq.HOUR_4,
                           atr=tp.SMAIndicator(period=162, column=tp.input("trend.tr"), shift=0),
                           supertrend=tp.SuperTrendIndicator(multiplier=1.5, volatility_column=tp.input("trend.atr"),

Questions this study answers

What was the question?

While the SuperTrend points one way, does holding with it or against it inside a session window control drawdown better than simply holding?

What did the selection period give?

A 10-strategy portfolio at USD 502 per unit: +USD 70,078 after cost from 2022-03 to 2025-12, max drawdown 4,723, worst day −1,523.

How did the validation period go?

The portfolio lost USD 2,564 from 2026-01 to 2026-10-23; of the 10 strategies only r14 stayed consistent with its drawdown control intact.

Why did it not hold?

The state signal has no mean edge; the selection profit came mostly from exit parameters chosen on the same data, whose contribution fell from +0.040 to +0.006 units per trade in validation.

What was decided?

The portfolio is not deployed; r14 is frozen as a new candidate and kept on watch, with a clean record from 2026-10-26.

Limitations

  • These are backtests; 2026 was read by earlier studies of the same instruments, so the validation is not a fully clean out-of-sample test.
  • Page statistics exclude swap; r13 and r14 are held strategies, and the internal calculations charged a 9% yearly swap on longs but not on shorts (r14).
  • r13 has only 65 trades, below the methodology’s 70.
  • r14 was picked after reading the validation period; its watch period is its first clean evidence.
