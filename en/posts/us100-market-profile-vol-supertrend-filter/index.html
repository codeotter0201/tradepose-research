Which SuperTrend filter, in which volatility? 36 US100 Market Profile setups

This is layer 2 of the Market Profile signals series: the trend filter. The previous article is layer 1, Can a delayed entry cut MAE?; this layer does not reuse its entries, but adds only a filter to the same 36 setups of the root study (immediate entry, held to 16:00), and layer 3 looks at the filter and the delayed entry together. G01–G16 are Market Profile event codes (for example, G10 = a whole bar closes above VAH); the glossary lists every condition, and P&L is in volatility mode. A trend filter is only worth keeping if it removes the right days, not just fewer days. This layer tests a SuperTrend veto: skip the day when the SuperTrend on 1 hour, 4 hours or the daily chart opposes the trade, either always or only in one volatility state. Instead of judging by eye, a rule fixed in advance counts, for each option, how many setups it improves beyond what random timing would.

Conclusion

The rule picks a different SuperTrend for each direction: longs are vetoed by the daily SuperTrend in every volatility state, shorts by the 1-hour SuperTrend only when the daily volatility level is low. The short choice is clear: it qualifies on 18 of 22 short setups. The long choice is weaker: two options tie at 9 of 14 long setups, only the median improvement separates them, and the daily veto skips about a third of the long days. The veto helps most on early-session shorts that were near zero or losing, and it hurts a few longs whose good days it removes.

  1. G10 short at 08:30 with the short veto more than halves its max drawdown, from −15.6 vol−28.9% to −7.0 vol−12.0%. Evidence: Risk
  2. G04 N1 long at 08:30 goes from 33 to 44 positive years of 55 with the long veto. Evidence: Consistency over time
  3. G12 short at 08:30 turns from a loss (profit factor 0.9570.911) into a gain (1.0340.963). Evidence: Returns

That is the conclusion. Each section below is the evidence for one claim; follow the sections or the evidence links into the strategy report for the data on every parameter set.

Why test against random timing?

Any veto that removes losing days looks good, and removing a random tenth of the days changes the result too. So each veto’s day mask is compared with the same mask shifted around the calendar, by every offset from 5% to 95% of the signal days: the same number of skipped days and the same runs of consecutive skips, only at the wrong times. A veto counts for a setup only if its recovery factor beats more than half of these shifts and beats the unfiltered entry.

What does this mean?

On these setups a SuperTrend filter is worth its keep for shorts in calm markets and, more weakly, for longs against a bearish daily SuperTrend in any volatility state, at the price of skipping about a third of the long days. Layer 3, filter attribution, combines the veto with the delayed entry and asks whether the SuperTrend adds anything beyond the volatility state itself.

Questions this study answers

Which SuperTrend timeframe should the filter use, and in which volatility state should it veto?

Longs use the daily SuperTrend and are vetoed in every volatility state; shorts use the 1-hour SuperTrend and are vetoed only when the daily volatility level is low. The short choice is clear (18 of 22 short setups qualify); the long choice is weaker, with two options tied at 9 of 14 and only the median change in recovery factor separating them.

Does the filter skip the right days, or just fewer days?

For early-session shorts it skips the right days: G10 short at 08:30 with the short veto cuts its max drawdown from −15.6 vol−28.9% to −7.0 vol−12.0%. For a few longs it skips good days: G03 long at 10:30 goes from a recovery factor of 1.072.31 to −0.120.64.

Questions raised along the way

Any veto that removes losing days looks good. How is luck ruled out?

Each veto’s day mask is compared with the same mask shifted around the calendar by every offset from 5% to 95% of the signal days, keeping the number and runs of skipped days. A veto counts for a setup only if its recovery factor beats more than half of these shifts and beats the unfiltered entry.

Two long options tie. How does the rule choose?

By the tie-break fixed in advance: the higher median change in recovery factor, which is the daily veto in every state (+0.26 against +0.07 for the 1-hour veto while volatility expands). Because a tie-break decides it, the long filter has weaker evidence than the short one, and layer 3 checks it again setup by setup.

When the veto improves a losing short, does it create an edge?

Only a thin one. Of the ten shorts that lose without a filter, five turn positive with the short veto, but with profit factors only between 1.005 and 1.047; three lose less but stay negative, and two lose more. G12 short at 08:30 is one of the five, going from a profit factor of 0.9570.911 to 1.0340.963.

Method and data

Research setup, data and calculations; the identifiers needed to reproduce the study are under data sources.

Research setup
Article type
Parameter sweep
Instrument
US100.cash
Direction
Both directions
Bars
US100 (FTMO) 15-minute execution, New York time; completed bars only
Setups
The 36 Market Profile setups of the root study, immediate entry, held to 16:00, every weekday
SuperTrend
One label per direction and timeframe, chosen earlier from capture efficiency and independent of the setups: long 1h ATR 345 × 3.5, 4h ATR 30 × 1.0, daily ATR 18 × 2.5; short 1h ATR 460 × 1.5, 4h ATR 120 × 1.0, daily ATR 24 × 1.0
Veto
Skip the day's trade when the SuperTrend opposes it (bearish for a long, bullish for a short), in every state or only in one daily volatility state
Volatility states
Level: daily ATR(24) ÷ previous close ranked over the last 168 days, top third high, bottom third low. Change: ATR(6) ÷ ATR(24), expanding above 1.1, contracting below 0.9
Rule
Per direction, the timeframe × state with the most setups that both beat more than half of the circular shifts of their day mask and raise the recovery factor; ties go to the higher median recovery change
Unit
Volatility mode: P&L divided by the previous completed New York daily ATR (72 daily bars)
Sample
New York entries from late January 2022 to September 23, 2026, Monday to Friday
Costs
Not included
Parameters
Signal: 18 values, full list in the strategy report; Direction: Long / Short; Entry time: 06:30 / 07:30 / 08:30 / 09:30 / 10:30; SuperTrend frequency: No filter / 1 hour / 4 hours / Daily; Volatility state: No filter / Every state / High level / Low level / Expanding / Contracting
Return calculation
Volatility-normalized: Sum of per-trade P&L ÷ volatility at exit (exit_volatility); Percentage: Sum of per-trade returns (simple)
