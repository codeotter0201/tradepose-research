Does US100 fall after SuperTrend turns bearish? A backtest of 71 settings over bearish spells

Many traders use a bearish SuperTrend as a filter: no longs while it points down. That only helps if US100 actually tends to fall during those bearish periods. This post is the root study of the SuperTrend state series and tests only that premise: every bearish period is one short, measured in volatility mode, that is, each trade’s P&L divided by the daily ATR at its entry, so a bearish period in a calm year counts as much as one in a volatile year. The next article in the series, Does the SuperTrend state carry information on US100?, turns the state into a condition for being long and adds entry and exit rules layer by layer.

How do we measure whether prices fall during a bearish period?

SuperTrend builds a channel from the Average True Range (ATR) times a multiplier; a close below the channel flips it bearish and a close back above flips it bullish. Each bearish period is represented by one short trade: enter when a completed SuperTrend bar flips bearish, exit when it flips back, with no stop loss, take profit or time exit. A profitable short means prices really did fall during that bearish period.

A bearish period can last days. Its result is divided by the daily ATR at entry and that divisor stays fixed until the exit, so a long period is measured against the volatility known when it started, not against whatever the volatility became later.

Does the Nasdaq fall after SuperTrend flips bearish?

Overall, no. The win rate is low for every setting: most bearish periods end at a loss when SuperTrend flips back, and the few large declines do not make up for them. The two settings above 1 are both 4-hour with multiplier 1.0, and both are barely above it.

Year by year, the bearish state did its job in 2022, when the index was in a real bear market, and failed in 2023 and 2026 to date: by the time SuperTrend turns bullish again in a rising market, the price has usually climbed back above where the short was entered.

Which timeframe is least bad?

Shorter timeframes lose less. 15- and 30-minute SuperTrends flip thousands of times and end close to break-even; 1-hour is below; 4-hour and daily lose clearly, because both the bearish flip and the flip back come late and give back the decline.

What does this mean?

On US100, a bearish SuperTrend is not a reliable sign that prices will fall. Using it to block longs would have helped in 2022 and cost in the other years; if a trend filter is needed, it should be judged by what it removes from the strategy it filters, against a random filter that blocks as many days, not by whether SuperTrend itself is right about direction. Whether the SuperTrend state works as a condition for being long is the question of the next article in the series, Does the SuperTrend state carry information on US100?.

No. Of 71 settings, only 20 have a profit factor above 1, and both barely. The bearish state caught the 2022 bear market but lost in the years after it, and the longer the timeframe, the worse it gets.

Questions raised along the way

A bearish period can last days: which volatility should its P&L be measured against?

The study divides each bearish period’s result by the daily ATR at entry and keeps that divisor fixed until the exit. A long period is therefore measured against the volatility known when it started, and the result is not distorted by how volatility changed during the trade.

The timeframes cover different parameter ranges: can they still be compared?

The 15- and 30-minute settings are the finalists of an earlier round and use smaller multipliers; the 1-hour, 4-hour and daily settings cover multipliers 1.0 to 4.0 in steps of 0.5. So the study compares multiplier and ATR length within each timeframe (the multiplier matters more), and across timeframes reads only the direction of the average profit factor: shorter timeframes lose less.

If the bearish state has no downward tendency, does the SuperTrend state carry any information?

This post tests only the short side. Whether the state picks better days to be long is left to the next article in the series, Does the SuperTrend state carry information on US100?, which compares the bullish and bearish states of the same setting.

Entry and exit
Short when SuperTrend flips bearish, cover when it flips back bullish; one trade per bearish period
Unit
Volatility mode: each trade's P&L divided by the New York daily ATR (72 daily bars, about 60 trading days) at entry, held fixed for the whole trade
Sample

Not included
Parameters
