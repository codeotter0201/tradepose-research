US100 clock windows by weekday × Market Profile: from 42 roots to a 15-strategy combination

A clock strategy enters and exits at fixed times. The question here is narrower: on which weekday does a clock window work, which Market Profile state makes it work, and how far can delayed entries and exits make it more robust, without asking for more than beating the market during exploration? Unless the page is switched, every number is in volatility mode: each trade’s P&L divided by the daily median True Range at exit (the median of each day’s True Range over the previous 143 New York sessions, about 0.92 times the daily ATR(72)), and every stop, target and delayed-entry distance is a multiple of it. A median rather than an ATR is used because it only looks at the last 143 daily bars and gives the same value whichever day the calculation starts from, so daily recalculation in live trading does not drift; see the research glossary.

Conclusion

42 roots (weekday × clock window × Market Profile gate) grew into 253 finished strategies. 195 beat the same-window market in the full sample and in both halves; after removing strategies that rise and fall together on the days they both trade and those whose edge does not cover trading costs, 15 strategies remain: Monday longs, Tuesday longs and shorts, Wednesday longs, Thursday shorts and a Friday short. For an FTMO 100K account the weekday multipliers are fixed by a rule that keeps them from swinging, and the worst possible day stays within 80% of the daily loss limit.

How was the research run?

Trading every session was tried first and only 10 combinations passed: the same window behaves very differently by weekday, and trading every session dilutes an edge that holds on only some days (details under “Questions raised along the way”). So this study puts the weekday back into every root and rescans the whole window grid, instead of adding a weekday condition to the every-session results.

  1. Roots by weekday. Each clock window is paired with no gate or one Market Profile gate and traded on a single weekday. A Market Profile gate looks at whether the entry bar opens above, inside or below the value area (the price range holding about 70% of the activity) of the two Profiles completed by entry time: the previous regular US session (RTH, 09:30-16:00 New York), the previous trading session, the current overnight session, or the first hour of the current regular session (the Initial Balance, IB). A cell only has to beat the market: its mean result per trade must exceed holding US100 long through the same window on every session of that weekday, over the full sample and in both halves. With tens of thousands of cells, a single-cell null lets many through by chance, so the null is family-wise: each random-day draw keeps the best window of the whole family (side × weekday × gate). Passing cells must form plateaus, and plateaus that correlate at 0.6 or more with a larger one are the same edge under another gate.
  2. One change per layer. Delayed entry, then a mandatory stop, take profit, breakeven and trailing stop. Each layer is compared only with its parent, and its grid comes from the parent’s own excursions. A favorable delayed entry places a Buy Limit below the signal price for longs or a Sell Limit above it for shorts and fills on a pullback; its distances stay below the winners’ MAE Q1 so most winners still fill. An adverse delayed entry places a Buy Stop above the signal price for longs or a Sell Stop below it for shorts and fills once price has moved first; its distances sit around the losers’ G_MFE Q2–Q3 where most losers never fill.
  3. Stops judged by MAE, not by a few trades. A stop must lower the mean MAE, sit at or above the MAE Q3 and outside the MAE cluster, and stop at MAE Q95: no stop is pushed into the tail to save a handful of high-MAE trades that end up winning. Trailing stops follow the same cluster rule on MHL. Entries and targets may cut a cluster.
  4. Pick in sample, confirm out of sample. Every layer ranks on 2022–2024; a pick survives only if 2025–2026 also beat the market and repeat the layer’s effect (a lower MAE, a higher mean for adverse delayed entries).
  5. Combination last, costs last. Correlation and profit room are only judged when every path is finished. Correlation uses only the sessions both strategies traded: each strategy trades on one weekday and often skips it, and the empty sessions would make two strategies look less related than they are.

Which weekdays carry the edge?

The family-wise null left 98 plateaus, and most are Monday longs and Tuesday shorts; after removing duplicates 42 roots remain. Thursday and Friday shorts and a few Tuesday and Wednesday longs complete the list. Monday longs beat holding by a small margin, because holding US100 long through those windows already earns; short windows beat it by a wide margin because holding loses through them.

What did the entry and exit layers change?

Most finished strategies keep a small favorable delayed entry: for 16 of the 42 roots no delayed entry passed, so they take the 0.02 fallback. In the final combination adverse delayed entries remain in a Monday long, a Tuesday short and a Thursday short, where waiting for the price to move against the trade first skips most losers. Every strategy has a stop; for some parents no stop met every rule and the closest one to the rules was used. Take profits and breakevens stay only where they lowered the MAE without raising the drawdown.

How large can the positions be on an FTMO 100K account?

Sizing follows Position sizing and risk allocation for an FTMO account: lots follow the stop distance so a stop-out costs a fixed amount; each weekday is one group whose multiplier comes from risk parity; and the worst case, every strategy of a day hitting its stop with slippage and cost, together with the backtest max drawdown, is checked against FTMO’s USD 4,500 daily and 10,000 total limits.

What sets these 15 strategies apart is how much the multipliers move: re-estimated every quarter on the trailing year, Thursday’s moved between 0.95 and 1.81. So the method is tightened here: only half of the estimate is used and the other half stays at every day equal; no group may sit more than 25% above or 20% below average; a multiplier changes only when a new estimate moves more than 20% away; and both limits are used only to 80%. The result: Monday 0.81, Tuesday 0.81, Wednesday 0.93, Thursday 1.20, Friday 1.25, unchanged since 2022, with USD 833 per stop-out × the day’s multiplier: about 675 on Monday and 1,000 on Thursday. Lots = that amount ÷ (stop multiple × the daily median True Range at entry), floored to 0.01 lot. These are this study’s figures for the 15 strategies; the registered portfolio was later cut to 14 strategies with recalculated multipliers, see US100 weekday clock portfolio for the current portfolio and sizing.

To check the design honestly, the multipliers and the base risk were set from 2022–2024 only and then run on 2025–2026:

The design used earns more than the history-only rule for nearly the same risk, never changes its multipliers, and keeps more room under the limits than giving every strategy the same stop-out. Switch the page to USD to see the whole period with these settings.

Before trading:Strategies with near stops trade many lots at typical volatility (around 21 lots for the Wednesday 20:30→22:30 long with a 0.14 stop), so check the broker’s lot and margin limits. Several strategies hold at the same time, sometimes long and short on the same instrument. The daily-limit check uses stop-outs; FTMO also counts floating losses during the day, which the next step checks on bar-level marks. The roots were chosen on the full sample; only the layers were confirmed out of sample. This is not trading advice.

What does this mean?

The weekday and the Market Profile state decide which sessions to take, delayed entries and exits only reduce risk, and a fixed set of weekday multipliers sizes the positions. The next step is to run the combination through FTMO’s intraday daily-loss rule on bar-level marks and to compare each quarter’s multiplier estimate with the fixed values before going live.

Questions this study answers

On which weekday and in which Market Profile state does a clock window work?

The family-wise null leaves 98 plateaus and 42 roots after removing duplicates; the robust edges sit on Monday longs and on Tuesday, Thursday and Friday shorts, with a few Tuesday and Wednesday longs. Monday longs beat holding by a small margin, because holding US100 through those windows already earns; short windows beat it by a wide margin because holding loses through them.

How far do delayed entries and exits make the windows more robust?

They mostly reduce risk rather than add return (the three findings in the conclusion are three examples); but not every layer improves every measure: for the Tuesday 14:30→16:00 long the stop and breakeven raised the max drawdown from −1.1 vol−1.9%−$941 to −1.6 vol−2.5%−$2,761.

How large can the 15 strategies trade together on an FTMO 100K account?

USD 833 per stop-out × the weekday multiplier (0.81 to 1.25, each day’s value in the section above), so a day with every stop hit stays within 80% of the daily limit. With the multipliers and base set from 2022–2024 only, 2025–2026 earned USD 72,394 with a max drawdown of 3,637 and no multiplier change. These are this study’s figures for the 15 strategies; the registered portfolio was later cut to 14 strategies with recalculated multipliers, see US100 weekday clock portfolio for the current portfolio and sizing.

Questions raised along the way

Why move from the every-session version to one weekday per root?

Traded every session, the clock tree’s 19 windows left only 10 robust combinations with a Market Profile gate; shorts that held on one weekday failed one of the halves, and ungated longs lost to the same-window hold every year. The study therefore built the weekday into every root and rescanned, turning a handful of candidates into 42 roots across all five weekdays.

With tens of thousands of cells, do many pass a random-day test by chance?

Yes, so the null is family-wise: each random-day draw takes the best cell of the whole side × weekday × gate family as the bar a cell must beat. Passing cells must also form plateaus on the time grid, and a plateau correlating at 0.6 or more with a larger one counts as the same edge and is dropped.

Why measure correlation only on the days both strategies trade?

Each strategy trades on one weekday and often skips it. Counting the days it does not trade as zero makes two strategies look unrelated even when they move together whenever both trade, so deduplication uses only the shared trading days, at least 20 of them.

Why is no SuperTrend filter applied?

On the finished every-session strategies, the SuperTrend × volatility veto removed trades that were profitable on average, failing the rule that removed trades must be losing ones, so no trend filter is applied here. The weekday, which did help, is built into every root.
